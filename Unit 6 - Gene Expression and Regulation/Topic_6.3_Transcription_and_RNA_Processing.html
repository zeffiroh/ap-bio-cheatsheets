Central Dogma & Functional Classes of RNA DNA → RNA → Protein
  • Central Dogma of Molecular Biology: Genetic information flows directionally from DNA (permanent blueprint) → RNA (transient intermediate via Transcription) → Polypeptide / Protein (functional machinery via Translation).
RNA Type Specific Molecular Role in Gene Expression
mRNA (Messenger RNA) Single-stranded transcript carrying triplet codons from DNA to the ribosome to specify the linear amino acid sequence of a polypeptide.
tRNA (Transfer RNA) Cloverleaf/L-shaped adaptor molecule carrying a specific amino acid at its 3′ end and a complementary 3-base anticodon loop that base-pairs with mRNA codons.
rRNA (Ribosomal RNA) Complexes with ribosomal proteins to form the large and small ribosomal subunits; acts as a catalytic ribozyme (peptidyl transferase) to form peptide bonds!
Don't Swap mRNA and tRNA Roles: mRNA carries the genetic message (codons); tRNA physically delivers amino acids (via anticodons) to the ribosome!
Template vs. Coding Strand & 3 Steps of Transcription Reads 3′→5′, Builds 5′→3′
  • Template Strand (also called Noncoding, Minus (–), or Antisense Strand):
    • The ONLY DNA strand physically read by RNA Polymerase in the 3′ → 5′ direction to build a complementary 5′ → 3′ RNA transcript (A→U, T→A, G↔C).
  • Coding Strand (also called Non-template, Plus (+), or Sense Strand):
    • Runs 5′ → 3′; has the exact same sequence and polarity as the newly synthesized mRNA except every T is replaced by U!
  • RNA Polymerase Properties:
    • Unwinds the DNA double helix locally by itself (no separate helicase needed) and does NOT require an RNA primer to initiate synthesis.
    • Synthesizes RNA strictly 5′ → 3′ by adding ribonucleoside triphosphates (NTPs) to the 3′-OH end.
  • Three Stages of Transcription:
    1. Initiation: RNA Polymerase binds to the promoter DNA sequence upstream of the gene (in eukaryotes, general transcription factors must first bind the promoter's TATA box to recruit RNA Pol II); DNA unwinds into a transcription bubble.
    2. Elongation: RNA Polymerase moves 3′ → 5′ along the template strand, elongating the RNA transcript 5′ → 3′ (with U pairing with A); DNA rewinds behind it.
    3. Termination: In prokaryotes, stops at a terminator hairpin sequence; in eukaryotes, RNA Pol II transcribes a polyadenylation signal (AAUAAA) and releases the pre-mRNA transcript.
Instant Strand Conversion Practice:
• DNA Coding (5′→3′): 5′ – A T G C C T A G T – 3′
• DNA Template (3′→5′): 3′ – T A C G G A T C A – 5′
• mRNA Transcript (5′→3′): 5′ – A U G C C U A G U – 3′
Always Check Which Strand Is Given! If an exam problem gives you the Coding (5′→3′) strand, simply swap T → U. Only take the complement if given the Template (3′→5′) strand!

Eukaryotic Pre-mRNA Processing & Prokaryote Comparison Eukaryotes Only!
  • In Eukaryotes, primary transcripts (pre-mRNA) undergo 3 mandatory covalent modifications inside the nucleus before export to the cytoplasm:
    1. 5′ Methyl-GTP Cap (5′ G-P-P-P): Added to the 5′ end; protects mRNA from hydrolytic 5′ exonucleases, aids export through nuclear pores, and helps the small ribosomal subunit recognize & bind the 5′ end.
    2. 3′ Poly-A Tail (50–250 Adenines): Added to the 3′ end by poly-A polymerase; increases mRNA stability/lifespan in the cytosol and facilitates nuclear export.
    3. RNA Splicing (via the Spliceosome / snRNPs): Excises non-coding intervening sequences (Introns) and splices together coding expressed sequences (Exons) to form continuous mature mRNA.
  • Alternative RNA Splicing: Different combinations of exons can be included or skipped from the same primary transcript → allows a single eukaryotic gene to code for multiple distinct polypeptide isoforms in different tissues!
  • Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes (High-Yield Distinction):
    • Prokaryotes: Lack a nuclear envelope and lack introns → NO RNA processing; ribosomes attach to mRNA while it is still being transcribed (coupled / simultaneous transcription & translation in the cytoplasm).
    • Eukaryotes: Nuclear envelope spatially & temporally separates transcription + RNA processing (in nucleus) from translation (in cytoplasm / rough ER).
Memory Hook: EXons are EXpressed (and EXit the nucleus in mature mRNA); INtrons are INtervening non-coding sequences that stay IN the nucleus (cut out and degraded)!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
