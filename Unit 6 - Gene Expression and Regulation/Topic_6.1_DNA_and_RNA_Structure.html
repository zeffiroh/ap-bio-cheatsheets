Genetic Material Organization: Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes Chromosomes & Plasmids
  • Universal Genetic Storage: DNA is the primary hereditary material in all cellular organisms (prokaryotes & eukaryotes); some viruses use RNA genomes (e.g., retroviruses like HIV, influenza, SARS-CoV-2).
  • Prokaryotic Genome:
    • Single circular double-stranded DNA chromosome located in the cytoplasmic nucleoid region (no nuclear envelope; no histone nucleosomes in bacteria).
  • Eukaryotic Genome:
    • Multiple linear DNA chromosomes housed inside the membrane-bound nucleus.
    • Negatively charged DNA (-PO₄²⁻) wraps tightly around positively charged histone proteins (rich in basic Lysine/Arginine) to form nucleosomes (“beads on a string”) → coils into chromatin → condenses into visible chromosomes during division.
  • Plasmids (Extrachromosomal DNA): Small, circular double-stranded DNA molecules found in bacteria (and rarely yeast/eukaryotes) that replicate independently, carry non-essential accessory genes (e.g., antibiotic resistance, toxin/metabolic genes), and can be transferred via conjugation or lab transformation.
Prokaryotes = Circular chromosome + Cytoplasmic nucleoid + Plasmids.
Eukaryotes = Linear chromosomes + Nucleus + Histone packaging (chromatin).
  • Chromatin vs. Chromosome: Same DNA + histone complex, just different packing/condensation states!
  • Plasmids ≠ Organelles: Plasmids are small naked circular DNA rings, not membrane-bound organelles.
Whenever an AP question mentions rapid spread of antibiotic resistance between bacteria or inserting a human gene into bacteria, immediately think Plasmids!
Classic Experiments Identifying DNA as Hereditary Material Experimental Design
Scientist(s) Experimental Setup & Discovery
Griffith (1928) Heat-killed virulent Smooth (S) S. pneumoniae mixed with live non-virulent Rough (R) bacteria transformed R cells into live, lethal S cells → discovered bacterial transformation.
Avery, MacLeod, McCarty (1944) Treated heat-killed S extract with protease, RNase, or DNase. Only DNase destroyed transforming ability → proved DNA is the transforming principle.
Hershey & Chase (1952) Bacteriophage T2 labeled with radioactive ³²P (DNA) vs. ³⁵S (Protein coat). Only ³²P entered bacterial pellet to direct new phage synthesis → confirmed DNA is viral genetic material.
Franklin, Watson & Crick (1953) Rosalind Franklin's X-ray diffraction (Photo 51) revealed helical dimensions; Watson & Crick built the antiparallel, complementary double-helix model.
Double Helix Polarity, Purines/Pyrimidines & Base Pairing 5′→3′ & H-Bonds
  • Nucleotide Monomer & Backbone: Consists of a 5′ phosphate group, a 5-carbon pentose sugar (2′-deoxyribose in DNA; ribose with 2′-OH in RNA), and a 1′ nitrogenous base. Monomers link via covalent phosphodiester bonds (3′-OH to 5′-phosphate) forming the exterior backbone; the linear base sequence encodes genetic information.
  • Antiparallel Double Helix: Two DNA strands run in opposite directions (one 5′ → 3′, the other 3′ → 5′) with hydrophilic sugar-phosphate backbones outside and hydrophobic bases stacked inside.
  • Purines (2 Rings) vs. Pyrimidines (1 Ring) & Chargaff’s Rules:
    • Purines (Double-ring): Adenine (A) and Guanine (G) — “Pure As Gold (2 rings)”.
    • Pyrimidines (Single-ring): Cytosine (C), Thymine (T, in DNA), Uracil (U, in RNA) — “CUT the Py (1 ring)”.
    • Complementary Base Pairing (Uniform Helix Diameter): A 2-ring purine always pairs with a 1-ring pyrimidine: A = T (or A = U in RNA) via 2 H-bonds; G ≡ C via 3 H-bonds (more G–C = higher thermal stability). Thus, %A = %T and %G = %C!
DNA vs. RNA Structural & Functional Comparison Master Blueprint vs. Copy
Feature DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)
Pentose Sugar Deoxyribose (lacks O at 2′ carbon: 2′-H) Ribose (has reactive hydroxyl at 2′ carbon: 2′-OH)
Strandedness Double-stranded (dsDNA) antiparallel helix Usually single-stranded (ssRNA); folds into 3D shapes (tRNA/rRNA) via internal base pairing
Nitrogenous Bases A, G, C, Thymine (T) A, G, C, Uracil (U)
Chemical Stability More stable (2′-H resists alkaline hydrolysis; double strand protects bases) Less stable / transient (2′-OH makes RNA prone to catalytic self-cleavage)
Cellular Role Long-term “master blueprint” storing hereditary info Temporary “working copy”: mRNA (codons), tRNA (amino acid carrier), rRNA (catalytic ribosome)
Why H-Bonds Matter: Hydrogen bonds between bases are weak individually (allowing helicase/RNA polymerase to unzip strands easily without breaking covalent phosphodiester bonds) but strong collectively across millions of base pairs!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
