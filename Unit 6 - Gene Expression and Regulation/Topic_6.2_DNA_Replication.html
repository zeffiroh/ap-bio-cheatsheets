Purpose & Semiconservative Nature of DNA Replication S Phase of Interphase
  • Purpose: Ensures faithful continuity of genetic information before cell division (mitosis/meiosis); occurs during S phase of interphase for growth, tissue repair, and reproduction.
  • Semiconservative Replication Model:
    • Parental double helix unwinds, and each original strand serves as a template for synthesizing a new complementary strand (A↔T, G↔C).
    • Each resulting daughter DNA molecule consists of 1 old (parental) strand + 1 newly synthesized strand.
  • Meselson-Stahl Experiment (1958): Cultured E. coli in heavy nitrogen (¹⁵N) then shifted to light nitrogen (¹⁴N). After Gen 1, 100% of DNA formed a single intermediate hybrid band (¹⁵N/¹⁴N, ruling out conservative); after Gen 2, 50% hybrid (¹⁵N/¹⁴N) + 50% light (¹⁴N/¹⁴N, ruling out dispersive).
Semiconservative = “Half Old, Half New”: Every replicated double helix preserves one intact parental template strand paired with one newly polymerized daughter strand.
5′ → 3′ Directionality & Leading vs. Lagging Strands Core AP Concept
  • Strict 5′ → 3′ Synthesis Rule:
    • DNA Polymerase can ONLY add incoming deoxynucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) to a pre-existing free 3′-OH (hydroxyl) end of a growing strand.
    • Therefore, new DNA is ALWAYS synthesized in the 5′ → 3′ direction while DNA Polymerase reads the template strand in the 3′ → 5′ direction.
    • Why Primers Are Required: DNA Polymerase cannot initiate a new polynucleotide chain from scratch — it requires Primase to lay down a short RNA primer that provides the initial free 3′-OH!
  • Because DNA is Antiparallel, the Two Strands Replicate Differently at a Replication Fork:
    • Leading Strand (3′ → 5′ Template): Synthesized continuously in one piece into (toward) the advancing replication fork; requires only 1 RNA primer.
    • Lagging Strand (5′ → 3′ Template): Synthesized discontinuously in short segments called Okazaki fragments moving away from the replication fork; requires a new RNA primer for every fragment, which are later replaced with DNA and covalently sealed by DNA Ligase.
Both Strands Are Synthesized 5′ → 3′: Never say the lagging strand is synthesized 3′ → 5′! Both leading and lagging strands are built strictly 5′ → 3′; the lagging strand simply builds short 5′ → 3′ Okazaki fragments oriented away from the opening fork.
Replication Accuracy, Proofreading & Telomeres High Fidelity
  • Proofreading & Mismatch Repair: DNA Polymerase checks each newly added base and uses 3′ → 5′ exonuclease activity to excise mismatched nucleotides immediately; mismatch repair enzymes and nucleotide excision repair (e.g., fixing UV thymine dimers) keep error rates near ~1 in 10¹⁰ bp.
  • End-Replication Problem in Eukaryotes: Linear chromosomes shorten at 5′ lagging ends when terminal RNA primers are removed; non-coding repetitive telomeres (TTAGGG) buffer coding genes, and telomerase extends telomeres in germ/stem cells (and cancer cells).

Master Table of DNA Replication Enzymes Must-Know CED Roles
Enzyme / Protein Specific Molecular Function at the Replication Fork
Topoisomerase (DNA Gyrase) Binds ahead of the replication fork; transiently nicks and reseals the sugar-phosphate backbone to relieve torsional strain and supercoiling caused by unwinding.
DNA Helicase Unwinds the parental double helix at the origin of replication by breaking hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs.
Single-Strand Binding Proteins (SSBPs) Coat separated single DNA strands to prevent them from snapping back together (re-annealing) or being degraded. (Helpful context; not tested by name alone)
Primase An RNA polymerase that synthesizes a short complementary RNA primer (5–10 nt) to provide a free 3′-OH group for DNA Polymerase to start.
DNA Polymerase (III & I) Elongates new DNA strands 5′ → 3′ ONLY by adding nucleotides to the 3′-OH end; proofreads new bases; replaces RNA primers with DNA nucleotides.
DNA Ligase Catalyzes covalent phosphodiester bonds to seal nicks in the sugar-phosphate backbone, joining Okazaki fragments into a continuous strand.
5-Word Enzyme Summary: Topoisomerase = untwists strain • Helicase = unzips H-bonds • Primase = lays first brick (RNA primer) • DNA Polymerase = builds 5′→3′ • Ligase = glues fragments!
Predict the Mutation / Inhibitor Question: AP FRQs love asking “What happens if Enzyme X is mutated or inhibited?”
  • Mutated Helicase: Strands cannot separate → no replication bubble forms.
  • Mutated Primase: No 3′-OH primer → neither leading nor lagging strand can start.
  • Mutated DNA Ligase: Leading strand finishes normally, but the lagging strand remains in unjoined Okazaki fragments!

Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
