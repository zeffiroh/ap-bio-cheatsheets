Gene Mutations: Point (Substitution) vs. Frameshift (Indels) Reading Frame Shifts
  • What Is a Mutation? Any permanent change in a cell's DNA nucleotide sequence. Mutations are the ultimate primary source of all new alleles and genetic variation in populations.
Mutation Class Molecular Change in Codons Effect on Polypeptide & Phenotype
Silent (Point Substitution) 1 base substituted → new codon codes for the exact same amino acid (due to degeneracy at 3rd wobble base). No change in primary amino acid sequence, protein folding, or phenotype.
Missense (Point Substitution) 1 base substituted → codon codes for a different amino acid (e.g., GAG→GUG Glu→Val in sickle-cell). Impact depends on R-group chemistry & active site location: conservative (similar R-group = minor) vs. non-conservative (polar ↔ nonpolar = misfolding!).
Nonsense (Point Substitution) 1 base substituted → sense codon converted into a premature Stop Codon (UAA, UAG, UGA). Terminates translation early → produces a truncated, shorter, usually nonfunctional polypeptide.
Frameshift (Insertion or Deletion) Addition or loss of nucleotides NOT in a multiple of 3 (e.g., ±1 or ±2 bases). Shifts the triplet reading frame → alters every downstream amino acid and usually hits an early stop codon (devastating loss of function).
  • Mutations in Non-Coding Regulatory Regions: Mutations in promoters, enhancers, silencers, or intron splice sites do not alter the coding triplet codons directly, but instead increase, decrease, or abolish the amount of protein produced (or alter exon splicing).
Silent Mutation ≠ “No Mutation”: A silent mutation is still a real DNA base-pair mutation (genotype changed); it simply has no effect on the amino acid sequence (phenotype unchanged) because of genetic code redundancy!
Sources of Mutations & Evolutionary Impact Random Mutation, Non-Random Selection
  • Endogenous Errors: Spontaneous DNA Polymerase replication errors that escape proofreading, or errors introduced during DNA repair/recombination.
  • Environmental Mutagens (Physical & Chemical):
    • UV Radiation: Causes covalent thymine dimers (T=T) that kink DNA and block replication/transcription unless fixed by nucleotide excision repair.
    • Ionizing Radiation (X-rays, Gamma rays): Generates free radicals that cause single- and double-stranded DNA backbone breaks.
    • Chemical Mutagens & Viruses: Base analogs, intercalating agents (cause frameshifts), or viral DNA insertion.
  • Beneficial, Neutral, or Detrimental Depends on Environment:
    • Whether a mutation is beneficial (increases fitness), neutral, or detrimental (decreases fitness) is determined by the selective environment (e.g., sickle-cell heterozygote advantage HbᴬHbˢ in malaria regions; antibiotic resistance in hospitals).
Golden Rule of Evolution on the AP Exam: Mutations occur completely at RANDOM — organisms NEVER mutate “on purpose” or “in response to a need” to survive an environmental challenge! Mutation is random; natural selection is non-random.

Chromosomal Changes & Prokaryotic Horizontal Gene Transfer Polyploidy & HGT
  • Large-Scale Chromosomal Structure & Number Mutations:
    • Structural Rearrangements: Deletion (loss of chromosomal segment, e.g., Cri-du-chat on Chr 5), Duplication (extra gene copies that can diverge into new functions like α/β-globin families), Inversion (180° reversal), and Translocation (segment moves between non-homologous chromosomes, e.g., Philadelphia chromosome in CML).
    • Aneuploidy vs. Polyploidy (from Nondisjunction): Aneuploidy = 1 extra or missing chromosome (2n±1); Polyploidy = entire extra sets of chromosomes (3n triploid, 4n tetraploid — common driver of rapid sympatric speciation and vigorous fruit size in plants like wheat, strawberries, and seedless bananas!).
  • Horizontal Gene Transfer (HGT) in Prokaryotes (Variation WITHOUT Meiosis):
    • 1. Transformation: Bacterium takes up naked foreign DNA fragments directly from the surrounding environment and incorporates them into its genome/plasmid.
    • 2. Transduction: Bacteriophages (viruses) accidentally package bacterial host DNA and inject it into a new bacterial host cell.
    • 3. Conjugation: Direct cell-to-cell transfer of a plasmid (e.g., F plasmid or R resistance plasmid) from donor (F⁺) to recipient (F⁻) through a cytoplasmic sex pilus (mating bridge).
    • 4. Transposition: “Jumping genes” (transposons) move from one genomic location/plasmid to another via transposase.
HGT 3-Word Key: Transformation = naked environmental DNA • Transduction = phage/virus delivery • Conjugation = bacterial sex pilus bridge!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
