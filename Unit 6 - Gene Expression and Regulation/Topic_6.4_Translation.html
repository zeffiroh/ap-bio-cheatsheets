Overview of Translation, Codons & Ribosome Sites mRNA → Polypeptide
  • Overview & Cellular Location: Translation decodes the nucleotide sequence of an mRNA transcript (5′ → 3′) into the amino acid sequence of a polypeptide (N-terminus → C-terminus). Occurs on ribosomes in the cytoplasm (prokaryotes & eukaryotes) or on the rough ER (eukaryotic secreted/membrane proteins).
  • Codons vs. Anticodons:
    • Codon (on mRNA): Non-overlapping triplet of 3 mRNA nucleotides read 5′ → 3′ that specifies 1 amino acid or a stop signal (4³ = 64 total codons: 61 sense + 3 stop).
    • Anticodon (on tRNA): 3-base sequence on the tRNA loop that base-pairs antiparallel and complementary to the mRNA codon (charged by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase using ATP).
    • Start Codon (5′-AUG-3′): Codes for Methionine (Met) (or formyl-Met in bacteria) and establishes the exact reading frame for all downstream triplets.
  • Ribosome Subunits & Three tRNA Binding Sites (A, P, E):
    • Small Subunit: Binds the mRNA transcript and aligns the start codon.
    • Large Subunit: Contains rRNA peptidyl transferase that catalyzes covalent peptide bonds; houses three binding sites:
      • A Site (Aminoacyl): Entry dock where the incoming charged aminoacyl-tRNA first arrives and binds the mRNA codon.
      • P Site (Peptidyl): Holds the tRNA carrying the growing polypeptide chain.
      • E Site (Exit): Exit chute where the uncharged (empty) tRNA is released back into the cytosol.
Ribosome Site Mnemonic: A = Arrive (Aminoacyl-tRNA enters) → P = Peptide (Polypeptide chain held) → E = Exit (Empty tRNA leaves)!
  • Codon vs. Anticodon Location: When using a Genetic Code codon chart on the AP Exam, ALWAYS look up the mRNA Codon (5′→3′), NEVER the tRNA anticodon or DNA template!
  • Example: mRNA start codon 5′-AUG-3′ pairs with tRNA anticodon 3′-UAC-5′ and codes for Methionine.
The Three Stages of Translation (Requires GTP Energy) Initiation → Termination
  • 1. Initiation: Small ribosomal subunit binds the mRNA (via 5′ cap in eukaryotes or Shine-Dalgarno rRNA binding site in bacteria) and scans to the AUG start codon. Initiator tRNA carrying Methionine (Met) binds AUG; the large subunit joins, placing Met-tRNA directly in the P site.
  • 2. Elongation (Cyclic 3-Step Loop):
    1. Codon Recognition: Incoming charged tRNA anticodon base-pairs with the mRNA codon in the A site.
    2. Peptide Bond Formation: Large subunit rRNA transfers the growing polypeptide from the P-site tRNA onto the amino acid in the A site (forming a peptide bond via dehydration synthesis).
    3. Translocation: Ribosome moves exactly 1 codon (3 nucleotides) forward (5′ → 3′) along the mRNA; the A-site tRNA shifts to the P site, and the empty P-site tRNA shifts to the E site and exits.
  • 3. Termination: Ribosome reaches one of the three Stop Codons (UAA, UAG, or UGA) in the A site. Stop codons do NOT code for any tRNA/amino acid; instead, a protein Release Factor binds the A site, hydrolyzes the completed polypeptide from the P-site tRNA, and causes the ribosomal subunits to dissociate.

Genetic Code Features, Retroviruses & Genotype→Phenotype Common Ancestry
  • Four Key Properties of the Genetic Code:
    • 1. Triplet & Non-Overlapping: Read in sequential groups of 3 bases without overlapping or gaps.
    • 2. Redundant / Degenerate: 61 sense codons code for only 20 amino acids → most amino acids are specified by multiple synonymous codons (usually differing at the 3rd “wobble” base, buffering against silent mutations).
    • 3. Unambiguous: Each specific codon codes for ONE and ONLY ONE specific amino acid (never two different amino acids!).
    • 4. Nearly Universal: Shared across virtually all bacteria, archaea, and eukaryotes (a human insulin gene is translated into functional human insulin inside E. coli) → powerful molecular evidence for universal common ancestry (LUCA)!
  • Retroviruses (Exception to Standard Information Flow):
    • RNA viruses such as HIV carry the viral enzyme Reverse Transcriptase, which synthesizes complementary DNA (RNA → cDNA) inside the host cell.
    • Viral DNA integrates into the host chromosome as a provirus (via integrase), and the host's own RNA polymerase and ribosomes then transcribe and translate viral proteins.
  • Tracing the Chain from Gene to Phenotype:
    DNA Base Sequence → mRNA Codons → Amino Acid Sequence (1°) → 3D Protein Folding (3°/4°) → Enzyme/Protein Function → Observable Phenotype.
Redundant vs. Ambiguous: The genetic code is redundant (several codons → 1 amino acid) but NEVER ambiguous (1 codon NEVER codes for multiple amino acids)!
Sickle-Cell Disease (Trace the Chain): Single base substitution (CTC → CAC) in the β-globin DNA gene → mRNA codon GAG → GUG → hydrophilic Glutamic Acid replaced by hydrophobic Valine → abnormal hemoglobin polymerizes under low O₂ → sickled red blood cells!

Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
