Restriction Enzymes & Gel Electrophoresis Size & Charge Separation
  • Restriction Enzymes (Endonucleases): Bacterial enzymes (e.g., EcoRI, BamHI) that recognize specific short palindromic DNA sequences (4–8 bp) and cleave phosphodiester bonds to produce staggered single-stranded overhangs called “sticky ends” (or blunt ends).
  • Gel Electrophoresis Principles:
    • Separates DNA, RNA, or protein fragments through a porous agarose/polyacrylamide gel matrix based on charge and molecular size (length in base pairs, bp).
    • Why DNA Moves Toward the Positive Electrode (Anode, +): DNA has a uniform net negative charge due to the phosphate groups (-PO₄²⁻) in its sugar-phosphate backbone. DNA is loaded into wells at the negative cathode (–) and migrates toward the positive anode (+) (“Run to Red”).
    • Size Sieving Rule: Smaller (shorter) DNA fragments navigate the gel pores easily and migrate FASTER and FARTHER from the wells; larger fragments move slowly and stay near the top.
  • Applications (DNA Fingerprinting / RFLP / STR Profiling): Forensics crime-scene matching, paternity testing (child's bands must be a 50/50 combination of mother + biological father), and evolutionary/medical allele diagnostics.
Gel Electrophoresis Rule: DNA is negatively charged (PO₄²⁻) → moves toward the positive (+) pole; Smaller fragments travel FARTHER! (Distance traveled is inversely proportional to log₁₀(bp)).
PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction): In Vitro DNA Amplification 2ⁿ Exponential Copies
  • Purpose: Rapidly amplifies a specific target DNA region into millions/billions of copies (2ⁿ after n cycles) in a thermal cycler without needing living cells.
  • 4 Required Reaction Components: (1) Target template DNA, (2) Forward & Reverse DNA primers (flank target sequence), (3) Free deoxynucleotides (dNTPs: A, T, C, G), and (4) Heat-stable Taq DNA Polymerase (isolated from thermophilic bacterium Thermus aquaticus so it doesn't denature at 95°C!).
  • Three Repeating Thermal Steps per Cycle:
    1. 1. Denaturation (~94–96°C): High heat breaks hydrogen bonds to separate double-stranded DNA into single strands (replaces Helicase!).
    2. 2. Annealing (~50–65°C): Reaction cools so forward and reverse DNA primers hydrogen-bond to complementary 3′ ends of the target sequence.
    3. 3. Extension / Elongation (~72°C): Optimal temp for Taq polymerase to synthesize new complementary DNA strands 5′ → 3′ starting from the primers.
Why PCR Uses Heat Instead of Helicase & DNA Primers Instead of Primase: Heating to 95°C denatures H-bonds physically (and would denature human DNA polymerase, which is why thermostable Taq polymerase is required), while synthetic DNA primers are added directly to the tube!

Recombinant DNA, Bacterial Transformation & Sequencing Cloning & CRISPR
  • Building Recombinant DNA & Bacterial Transformation:
    • 1. Cut with the SAME Restriction Enzyme: Cut both the bacterial plasmid vector and the foreign DNA (gene of interest) with the exact same restriction enzyme so their complementary sticky ends hydrogen-bond together.
    • 2. Ligate with DNA Ligase: Add DNA Ligase to form covalent phosphodiester bonds, sealing the foreign gene into the recombinant plasmid.
    • 3. Why Use Eukaryotic cDNA (Complementary DNA)? Because bacteria lack spliceosomes and cannot remove introns, scientists use reverse transcriptase on mature eukaryotic mRNA to make intron-free cDNA (plus a bacterial promoter) before inserting a human gene (like human insulin) into E. coli!
    • 4. Selection on Antibiotic Plates (ampᴿ / GFP / lacZ): Heat-shock CaCl₂-treated competent bacteria and plate on agar containing ampicillin. Only bacteria that successfully took up the plasmid carrying the ampicillin-resistance gene (ampᴿ) survive and form colonies!
  • DNA Sequencing & CRISPR-Cas9 Genome Editing:
    • DNA Sequencing: Determines the exact nucleotide order (A, T, C, G) of a DNA fragment to identify disease mutations, build phylogenetic trees, and map genomes.
    • CRISPR-Cas9: Uses a synthetic guide RNA (gRNA) complementary to a target DNA sequence to direct the Cas9 endonuclease to cut a specific genomic locus to knock out or repair a gene.
Circular vs. Linear Restriction Digest Math on AP Exams:
  • Cutting a LINEAR DNA molecule n times produces n + 1 fragments (bands).
  • Cutting a CIRCULAR plasmid n times produces n fragments (bands)!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
