Promoters, Enhancers, Silencers & Transcription Factors Eukaryotic Initiation
  • Promoters & General Transcription Factors:
    • Every eukaryotic gene has a proximal promoter (often containing a conserved TATA box ~25–30 bp upstream of the transcription start site).
    • General Transcription Factors (TFs) bind the TATA box required for basal (low-level) transcription of all protein-coding genes and recruit RNA Polymerase II to form the transcription initiation complex.
  • Distal Enhancers & Specific Activators (Positive Regulation):
    • Enhancers are distal regulatory DNA sequences (located thousands of base pairs upstream, downstream, or even within an intron) composed of multiple control elements.
    • Specific Activator proteins bind enhancers; a DNA-bending protein folds the DNA into a hairpin loop, bringing the bound activators into contact with Mediator proteins, general TFs, and RNA Pol II at the promoter → dramatically boosts transcription rate!
  • Silencers & Repressors (Negative Regulation):
    • Silencers are regulatory DNA sequences that bind Repressor proteins.
    • Repressors decrease or shut off transcription by blocking activator binding, preventing RNA Polymerase recruitment, or recruiting histone deacetylases to condense chromatin.
Enhancers + Activators = Boost Transcription (Gas Pedal via DNA Looping).
Silencers + Repressors = Block Transcription (Brake Pedal / Negative Control).
Differential Gene Expression & Cell Specialization Same Genome, Unique Proteome
  • Genomic Equivalence: Virtually every somatic cell in a multicellular organism arises from the same fertilized zygote via mitosis and therefore contains the exact same complete DNA genome.
  • Why Cells Differ (Differential Gene Expression): Different cell types express different subsets of genes (alongside shared “housekeeping” genes like glycolysis enzymes, actin, and histones) because each cell type contains a unique cytoplasmic combination of specific transcription factors (activators/repressors)!
  • Sequential Expression in Embryonic Development:
    • Cytoplasmic Determinants (e.g., maternal Bicoid mRNA): Unevenly distributed maternal mRNAs/proteins in the unfertilized egg establish early body axes (anterior-posterior).
    • Embryonic Induction & Homeotic (Hox) Genes: Signaling molecules (morphogens) secreted by neighboring embryonic cells trigger cascades of master transcription factors (such as Hox genes and MyoD) that lock in tissue identity.
Liver Cell vs. Lens Cell (or Muscle vs. Neuron): Both a liver hepatocyte and an eye lens cell possess the genes for albumin and crystallin, but only liver cells make the specific activators for albumin, while only lens cells make the specific activators for crystallin!
Differentiation ≠ Loss or Change of DNA: Specialized cells (like neurons, muscle cells, and skin cells) differ because of differential gene expression (which mRNAs/proteins are made), NOT because they have different DNA sequences!

Post-Transcriptional Control: Small RNAs (miRNA & siRNA) RNA Interference (RNAi)
  • Three Core Mechanisms Driving Differential Gene Expression:
    1. Epigenetic Chromatin Modifications: Histone acetylation (ON) vs. DNA methylation (OFF).
    2. Cell-Specific Transcription Factors: Activators & repressors binding enhancers/silencers.
    3. Post-Transcriptional RNA Processing & Small RNAs: Alternative splicing + RNA interference (RNAi).
  • Small Non-Coding RNAs (RNA Interference / RNAi): Short (~21–23 nt) single-stranded RNA molecules that associate with protein complexes (RISC) in the cytoplasm to base-pair with target mRNAs and prevent translation:
Small RNA Type Complementary Pairing Mechanism of Post-Transcriptional Silencing
miRNA (microRNA) Binds partially complementary sequences on target mRNA Physically blocks ribosome translation (or triggers deadenylation & mRNA decay); fine-tunes endogenous developmental genes.
siRNA (small interfering RNA) Binds perfectly (100%) complementary mRNA sequence Triggers immediate enzymatic cleavage & degradation of the target mRNA (evolved as an antiviral defense against double-stranded viral RNA).
Diagnosing High mRNA with Zero Protein on the AP Exam: If an experiment shows that a cell has high levels of mature cytoplasmic mRNA for Gene X, yet produces ZERO Protein X, look for miRNA / siRNA post-transcriptional silencing blocking translation or degrading the mRNA!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
