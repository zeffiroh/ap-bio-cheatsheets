Why Regulate Genes? Regulatory Sequences & Proteins Right Time, Place, Amount
  • Purpose of Gene Regulation: Conserves cellular energy/resources in prokaryotes and allows multicellular eukaryotes with the exact same genome to differentiate into distinct cell types by expressing proteins at the right time, right place, and right amount.
  • Regulatory DNA Sequences (cis-acting elements on DNA):
    • Promoter: DNA sequence upstream of a gene where RNA Polymerase and general transcription factors bind to initiate transcription.
    • Operator (Prokaryotes): DNA switch segment within/near the promoter that binds a repressor protein to block RNA Polymerase.
    • Enhancers & Silencers (Eukaryotes): Distal control regions (often thousands of bp away) that bind activators (boost transcription) or repressors (decrease transcription).
  • Regulatory Proteins (trans-acting factors):
    • Activators (Positive Control): Bind enhancers/promoters to recruit RNA Polymerase → turn transcription ON / UP.
    • Repressors (Negative Control): Bind operators/silencers to block RNA Polymerase → turn transcription OFF / DOWN.
Activators = ON (Gas Pedal) • Repressors = OFF (Brake Pedal).
Epigenetic Chromatin Regulation (Euchromatin vs. Heterochromatin) No DNA Sequence Change
  • Epigenetics: Heritable, reversible chemical modifications to DNA or histone proteins that alter chromatin packing and gene accessibility without altering the DNA base sequence.
  • 1. Histone Acetylation (-COCH₃):
    • Neutralizes positive charges on histone Lysine tails → weakens grip on negatively charged DNA → loosens chromatin into open Euchromatin → INCREASES transcription (Gene ON).
  • 2. DNA Methylation (-CH₃ on Cytosine bases):
    • Adds methyl groups directly to promoter cytosines (CpG islands) and recruits deacetylases → condenses chromatin into tightly packed Heterochromatin → SILENCES transcription (Gene OFF) (e.g., X-chromosome Barr body inactivation, genomic imprinting).
Chromatin State Packing & Chemical Tags Transcription Level
Euchromatin Loose / Open (High Histone Acetylation, Low DNA Methylation) HIGH (Active ON) — RNA Polymerase can access promoters
Heterochromatin Tight / Condensed (High DNA Methylation, Histone Deacetylation) LOW / OFF (Silenced) — Promoters physically blocked
Memory Hook: Histone Acetylation = Active / Accessible (Euchromatin ON); DNA Methylation = Muted (Heterochromatin OFF)!
Eukaryotic Multi-Level & Post-Translational Control No Operons in Eukaryotes
  • Coordinated Eukaryotic Regulation: Eukaryotes do NOT have operons; instead, genes in the same pathway located on different chromosomes share the same combination of regulatory control elements so a single transcription factor activates all of them simultaneously.
  • Post-Translational Modifications (After Protein Synthesis):
    • Phosphorylation: Kinases add -PO₄³⁻ (phosphatases remove it) to rapidly switch enzyme activity ON or OFF.
    • Ubiquitination: Attaches ubiquitin tags to damaged or unneeded proteins, targeting them for destruction inside the proteasome.
    • Glycosylation / Cleavage: Adds carbohydrate tags for membrane targeting or cleaves pro-proteins (e.g., pro-insulin → active insulin).
Prokaryotic Operons: Inducible (lac) vs. Repressible (trp) Master Bacterial Control
  • What Is an Operon? A bacterial cluster of functionally related structural genes transcribed together as a single polycistronic mRNA under the control of 1 Promoter and 1 Operator (plus an upstream regulatory gene encoding the repressor protein).
Feature Inducible Operon (e.g., lac Operon) Repressible Operon (e.g., trp Operon)
Default State Normally OFF (Repressor synthesized in active shape bound to Operator) Normally ON (Repressor synthesized in inactive shape; cannot bind alone)
Metabolic Pathway Catabolic (Breakdown): Makes enzymes (β-galactosidase) ONLY when nutrient is present Anabolic (Synthesis): Makes enzymes to build amino acid UNTIL product accumulates
Effector Molecule Role Inducer (Allolactose / Lactose): Binds repressor → allosterically inactivates repressor → repressor falls off operator → Turns Operon ON! Corepressor (Tryptophan): Binds inactive repressor → allosterically activates repressor to bind operator → Turns Operon OFF!
Positive Control (cAMP–CAP) When Glucose is LOW, high cAMP binds CAP activator at promoter → HIGH transcription (only when Lactose PRESENT + Glucose ABSENT). Regulated by negative corepressor feedback (and ribosome attenuation).
Classic AP Operon Mutation Scenarios:
  • Mutated Operator (cannot bind repressor) OR Mutated Regulatory Gene (lacI⁻): Repressor can never bind → operon is constitutively ON (always transcribed)!
  • Mutated Promoter: RNA Polymerase can never bind → operon is permanently OFF!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 8 & 9 (pGLO Bacterial Transformation & Restriction Enzyme Analysis of DNA): Heat-shock transformation of E. coli with pGLO plasmid (bla ampicillin-resistance gene + GFP under arabinose-inducible araC promoter). Plate comparisons: −pGLO/LB (lawn, viability control), −pGLO/LB+Amp (no growth, antibiotic lethality control), +pGLO/LB+Amp (white colonies, transformation), +pGLO/LB+Amp+Ara (glowing green colonies under UV, inducible gene expression). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Transformation Efficiency = (Total colonies counted on selective plate) / (μg of plasmid DNA spread). Gel Electrophoresis Semi-Log Standard Curve: DNA fragment size (bp, log₁₀ scale on y-axis) vs. migration distance (mm, linear scale on x-axis) yields a straight line for interpolating unknown fragment lengths. PCR amplification after n thermal cycles yields 2ⁿ target DNA copies. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Splice-site mutations in the β-globin gene (β-Thalassemia) prevent the spliceosome (snRNPs) from excising an intron or cause exon skipping, shifting the mRNA reading frame and introducing a premature nonsense stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA). In oncology, epigenetic hypermethylation of CpG islands in tumor-suppressor promoters (e.g., BRCA1 or MLH1) or histone deacetylation condenses euchromatin into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin without altering the primary DNA sequence.
