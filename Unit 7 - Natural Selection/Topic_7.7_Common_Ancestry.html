Universal Common Ancestry (LUCA): All Life on Earth Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya
  • Structural and functional evidence conserved across all three domains of life (Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya) demonstrates that all living organisms descended from a single Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA):
  • 6 Universal Molecular Homologies (Shared by ALL Life):
    • 1. DNA & RNA as Genetic Material: Double-stranded DNA stores hereditary information and RNA transcripts express it via base-pairing (A-T/U, G-C).
    • 2. Nearly Universal Genetic Code: The same 64 mRNA codons specify the exact same 20 amino acids from bacteria to humans (enabling transgenic biotechnology, e.g., bacteria expressing human insulin!).
    • 3. Ribosomes (rRNA + proteins): All cells translate mRNA into polypeptides using ribosomes.
    • 4. Core Metabolic Pathways (Glycolysis): Cytosolic glycolysis converts glucose to pyruvate without O₂ across virtually all organisms, showing it evolved very early before Earth had an oxygenated atmosphere.
    • 5. ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) & Chemiosmosis: Universal cellular energy currency and proton-gradient ATP synthase machinery.
    • 6. Phospholipid Bilayer Cell Membranes: Selectively permeable amphipathic lipid membranes enclose all cells.
Fundamental Evidence for Common Ancestry of Eukaryotes CED EVO-2.A Focus
  • All eukaryotes (protists, fungi, plants, and animals) share specific derived structural and molecular features not found in Bacteria, proving inheritance from a single common eukaryotic ancestor (LECA):
  • 1. Membrane-Bound Organelles & Nucleus:
    • All eukaryotes possess internal membrane-bound compartments (nucleus, ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes) that compartmentalize incompatible cellular processes and increase surface-area-to-volume efficiency.
    • Mitochondria are present in all eukaryotic lineages (or their vestigial remnants, mitosomes/hydrogenosomes), reflecting a single ancient endosymbiotic uptake of an aerobic alpha-proteobacterium.
  • 2. Multiple Linear Chromosomes + Histone Proteins:
    • Eukaryotic nuclear DNA is organized into multiple linear chromosomes wrapped around histone spools (nucleosomes) and capped by telomeres, enclosed within a double nuclear envelope.
    • Contrast: Prokaryotes typically have a single circular chromosome located in an unbound cytoplasmic nucleoid region.
  • 3. Genes Containing Introns & Spliceosomal RNA Processing:
    • Eukaryotic protein-coding genes are interrupted by non-coding introns interspersed with coding exons.
    • Pre-mRNA undergoes 5′ GTP capping, 3′ poly-A tailing, and spliceosome-mediated intron removal (enabling alternative splicing) inside the nucleus before translation.
Shared mitochondria = evidence of a shared eukaryotic lineage. Mitochondria evolved before the endosymbiosis of chloroplasts (which occurred later only in the photosynthetic plant/algal lineage).
Why Introns & Linear Chromosomes Matter: Both are major structural synapomorphies shared across diverse eukaryotic kingdoms, strongly supporting a single monophyletic origin of Domain Eukarya.
Do not confuse introns with exons! Introns (intervening sequences) are removed/spliced out; Exons (expressed sequences) remain and exit the nucleus to be translated into protein!

Scholarly Dimension Comprehensive Academic Reference & Mechanistic Specification
I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 1, 2 & 3 (Artificial Selection, Hardy-Weinberg Mathematical Modeling & BLAST Bioinformatics): Comparing allele frequency shifts across generations under directional selection vs. small-population genetic drift simulations, and constructing maximum-parsimony cladograms from BLAST percentage sequence identity matrices (Cytochrome c / β-globin). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Golden Rule: Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE): Allele frequencies p + q = 1; Genotype frequencies p² (homozygous dominant) + 2pq (heterozygous carriers) + q² (homozygous recessive) = 1. Golden Calculation Sequence: ALWAYS begin with the recessive phenotype frequency (q²), take √(q²) to find q, compute p = 1 − q, and then calculate 2pq (heterozygote carrier frequency). Never take the square root of the dominant phenotype frequency (p² + 2pq)! Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Evolutionary & Biomedical Disruption Case Study: Incomplete antibiotic regimens or agricultural antibiotic overuse exert strong directional selection on bacterial populations (e.g., MRSA or MDR-Tuberculosis): susceptible cells die while rare, pre-existing resistant mutants (arising via random mutation or R-plasmid conjugation) survive and reproduce. In conservation biology, severe population bottlenecks (e.g., Northern Elephant Seals, African Cheetahs) drastically erode genetic diversity via genetic drift, increasing homozygosity for deleterious recessive alleles and vulnerability to novel pathogens.
