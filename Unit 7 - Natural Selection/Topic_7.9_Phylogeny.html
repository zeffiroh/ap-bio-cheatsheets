Key Terms & Formal Definitions Lexicon — Topic 7.9 (Phylogeny: Phylogenetic Trees, Cladograms & Clades) CED Core Vocabulary
Phylogeny
The evolutionary history and line of descent of a species or group of related organisms.
Phylogenetic Trees vs. Cladograms
Cladogram: Shows branching order (relative relatedness) only based on shared derived characters; branch lengths do not represent time or amount of genetic change.
Phylogenetic Tree
Branch lengths are frequently calibrated to represent geological time (via fossils/molecular clocks) or the amount of genetic change (nucleotide substitutions).
Key Structural Components
Root: The basal lineage representing the oldest common ancestor of all taxa on the tree.
Nodes (Branch Points)
Represent speciation events and the Most Recent Common Ancestor (MRCA) of the descendant lineages.
Tips (Terminal Taxa)
Extant (living) or extinct species at the ends of branches.
Sister Taxa
Two lineages that diverge from the exact same immediate node — they share an MRCA not shared with any other group and are each other's closest relatives.
Outgroup
A distantly related lineage that diverged before the ingroup; serves as a reference point to distinguish ancestral vs. derived traits.
1. Anatomy of Phylogenetic Trees & Cladograms Evolutionary Hypotheses
  • Phylogeny: The evolutionary history and line of descent of a species or group of related organisms.
  • Phylogenetic Trees vs. Cladograms:
    • Cladogram: Shows branching order (relative relatedness) only based on shared derived characters; branch lengths do not represent time or amount of genetic change.
    • Phylogenetic Tree: Branch lengths are frequently calibrated to represent geological time (via fossils/molecular clocks) or the amount of genetic change (nucleotide substitutions).
  • Key Structural Components:
    • Root: The basal lineage representing the oldest common ancestor of all taxa on the tree.
    • Nodes (Branch Points): Represent speciation events and the Most Recent Common Ancestor (MRCA) of the descendant lineages.
    • Tips (Terminal Taxa): Extant (living) or extinct species at the ends of branches.
    • Sister Taxa: Two lineages that diverge from the exact same immediate node — they share an MRCA not shared with any other group and are each other's closest relatives.
    • Outgroup: A distantly related lineage that diverged before the ingroup; serves as a reference point to distinguish ancestral vs. derived traits.
Tree = Time/Change + Relationships  |  Cladogram = Branching Relationships Only.
Rotating Around a Node Does NOT Change Relationships! Never read across the tips from left to right to judge relatedness — ONLY look at how recently two taxa share a common node (MRCA)!
2. Synapomorphies, Clades & Groupings Monophyletic Clades
  • Shared Ancestral Character (Symplesiomorphy): A trait that originated in an ancestor prior to the clade of interest (e.g., backbone in mammals — shared with all vertebrates).
  • Shared Derived Character (Synapomorphy): An evolutionary novelty unique to a specific clade, inherited from its MRCA (e.g., hair & mammary glands in mammals). Traits can be gained or secondarily lost (e.g., legs lost in snakes) over evolution.
  • Three Types of Taxonomic Groupings:
    • Monophyletic Group (True Clade): Includes a single common ancestor (node) and ALL of its descendants ("one-snip test").
    • Paraphyletic Group: Includes a common ancestor and some, but not all, of its descendants (e.g., traditional "Reptilia" excluding birds).
    • Polyphyletic Group: Groups distantly related organisms without their common ancestor (often based on convergent analogous traits, e.g., "warm-blooded animals" = birds + mammals).
Shared derived traits (synapomorphies) = evolutionary "clues" linking species into clades.
3. Building Trees: Molecular Data & Maximum Parsimony CED EVO-3.C
  • Maximum Parsimony (Occam's Razor): When choosing among competing phylogenetic trees, scientists prefer the tree that requires the fewest total evolutionary events (fewest mutations or character gains/losses).
  • Why Molecular Data Beats Morphology Alone:
    • Morphological traits can be misleading due to convergent evolution (analogous structures) or extreme divergence.
    • DNA, RNA, and protein sequences provide thousands of objective nucleotide positions and reveal true evolutionary relationships even when morphology converges.
  • Phylogenetic Trees Are Hypotheses: Trees are constantly revised as new fossils or genome sequences are discovered!

3-Step Recipe for AP FRQ Cladogram Drawing:
  • 1. Identify the Outgroup: The species with all 0s (or highest number of amino acid differences) goes on the first basal branch closest to the root.
  • 2. Group Sister Taxa: Place the two species with the most shared derived traits (1s) or fewest DNA differences together on the most recent split.
  • 3. Place Character Hash Marks: Place each trait hash mark on the internode just below the node uniting all species that possess that trait!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 1, 2 & 3 (Artificial Selection, Hardy-Weinberg Mathematical Modeling & BLAST Bioinformatics): Comparing allele frequency shifts across generations under directional selection vs. small-population genetic drift simulations, and constructing maximum-parsimony cladograms from BLAST percentage sequence identity matrices (Cytochrome c / β-globin). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Golden Rule: Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE): Allele frequencies p + q = 1; Genotype frequencies p² (homozygous dominant) + 2pq (heterozygous carriers) + q² (homozygous recessive) = 1. Golden Calculation Sequence: ALWAYS begin with the recessive phenotype frequency (q²), take √(q²) to find q, compute p = 1 − q, and then calculate 2pq (heterozygote carrier frequency). Never take the square root of the dominant phenotype frequency (p² + 2pq)! Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Evolutionary & Biomedical Disruption Case Study: Incomplete antibiotic regimens or agricultural antibiotic overuse exert strong directional selection on bacterial populations (e.g., MRSA or MDR-Tuberculosis): susceptible cells die while rare, pre-existing resistant mutants (arising via random mutation or R-plasmid conjugation) survive and reproduce. In conservation biology, severe population bottlenecks (e.g., Northern Elephant Seals, African Cheetahs) drastically erode genetic diversity via genetic drift, increasing homozygosity for deleterious recessive alleles and vulnerability to novel pathogens.
