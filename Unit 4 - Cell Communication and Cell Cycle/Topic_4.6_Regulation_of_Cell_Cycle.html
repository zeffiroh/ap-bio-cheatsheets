The 3 Major Internal Cell Cycle Checkpoints Quality Control Gates
  • Purpose of Checkpoints: Internal molecular surveillance mechanisms that pause the cell cycle to verify fidelity and prevent damaged or unprepared cells from dividing. Ensure stages occur in the correct order, at the right time, and only once per cycle.
Checkpoint Timing What the Cell Inspects If Checkpoint Fails
1. G₁ Checkpoint
(Restriction Point)		 Near end of G₁ (BEFORE DNA replication) Adequate cell size, sufficient nutrients/ATP, external growth factors, and undamaged DNA. Pauses cycle for DNA repair, exits into non-dividing G₀ phase, or undergoes apoptosis.
2. G₂ Checkpoint End of G₂ (AFTER S-phase replication, before Mitosis) Verifies DNA replication is 100% complete, newly replicated DNA is undamaged, and MPF levels are sufficient. Arrests at G₂ to activate DNA repair enzymes; if irreparable → triggers apoptosis.
3. M (Spindle) Checkpoint During Metaphase (before Anaphase) Ensures ALL chromosomes' kinetochores are properly attached to spindle microtubules from opposite poles. Blocks separase activation (delays Anaphase) to prevent nondisjunction / aneuploidy!
Two High-Yield Checkpoint Mistakes:
1. G₁ checks DNA BEFORE replication (to avoid copying mutated templates), whereas G₂ checks DNA AFTER replication (before mitosis begins).
2. At the M checkpoint, if even a single chromosome is unattached to spindle fibers, the cell must NOT proceed from Metaphase to Anaphase!
Molecular Engine: Cyclins & Cyclin-Dependent Kinases (CDKs) CDK = Engine | Cyclin = Key
  • Cyclin-Dependent Kinases (CDKs):
    • Enzymes present at a constant concentration throughout the entire cell cycle, but they are catalytically INACTIVE on their own.
  • Cyclins:
    • Regulatory proteins whose concentrations oscillate (rise and fall cyclically) across the cell cycle as they are synthesized and degraded.
  • How Cyclin–CDK Control Works (e.g., MPF — Maturation Promoting Factor):
    1. Synthesis & Binding: As a cell grows in S/G₂, mitotic cyclin accumulates and binds allosterically to CDK → forms the active Cyclin–CDK complex (MPF).
    2. Phosphorylation of Targets: Active CDK uses ATP to phosphorylate target proteins (e.g., nuclear lamins to fragment the nuclear envelope, condensins to condense chromosomes, spindle proteins) → pushes cell past the G₂ checkpoint into Mitosis.
    3. Ubiquitin-Proteasome Degradation: In late mitosis (Anaphase), the cyclin subunit is enzymatically degraded → CDK becomes inactive again → cell exits mitosis into G₁.
External Signals, Cancer & Loss of Checkpoint Control Uncontrolled Division
  • External Regulatory Signals in Normal Cells:
    • Growth Factors (e.g., PDGF): Paracrine ligands secreted by platelets/cells that bind RTK receptors to stimulate target cells to pass the G₁ checkpoint and divide.
    • Density-Dependent Inhibition (Contact Inhibition): Crowded cells touch one another and stop dividing once a single confluent monolayer fills a surface.
    • Anchorage Dependence: Normal animal cells must be physically attached to an extracellular matrix (ECM) substratum via integrins in order to divide.
  • Hallmarks of Cancer Cells (Failure of Cell Cycle Regulation):
    • Ignore internal checkpoints (G₁, G₂, M) and continue dividing even with severe DNA damage.
    • Make their own growth factors (autocrine loop) or have constitutively active receptors/kinases.
    • Lose density-dependent inhibition (pile up into tumors) and lose anchorage dependence (metastasize through blood/lymph).
    • Evade apoptosis and express telomerase for replicative immortality.
Proto-Oncogenes vs. Tumor Suppressor Genes (p53) Gas Pedal vs Brakes
Gene Class Normal Cellular Function Cancer-Causing Mutation & Analogy
Proto-Oncogenes → Oncogenes
(e.g., Ras, Myc, HER2)		 Normally promote cell growth and division in a regulated way in response to growth factors. Gain-of-Function (Dominant — 1 mutant allele needed): Converts proto-oncogene into an overactive oncogene.
Analogy = "Gas pedal stuck down!"
Tumor Suppressor Genes
(e.g., p53, BRCA1/2, Rb)		 Normally inhibit cell division, repair damaged DNA, or trigger apoptosis when DNA is irreparable. Loss-of-Function (Recessive — 2 mutant alleles needed): Removes normal checkpoint inhibition.
Analogy = "Cut the brakes!"
  • Spotlight on p53 ("Guardian of the Genome" — Mutated in >50% of Human Cancers):
    • When DNA damage (UV, radiation, replication error) is detected at the G₁ checkpoint, the p53 transcription factor is activated and performs 3 protective actions:
      1. Halts the Cell Cycle at G₁: Transcribes p21, a CDK-inhibitor protein that blocks Cyclin–CDK so the cell has time to repair its DNA.
      2. Activates DNA Repair Enzymes to fix the lesion.
      3. Triggers Apoptosis (Programmed Cell Death): If DNA damage is too severe to repair, p53 activates caspase apoptosis genes so the mutated cell destroys itself.
    • Consequence of Defective/Mutated p53: Cells with damaged DNA fail to pause or undergo apoptosis → survive, replicate damaged DNA, and accumulate mutations → cancer!
AP Exam Scope Note: You do NOT need to memorize the names of specific cyclin-CDK pairs (like Cyclin B / CDK1) or specific growth factors — focus on how cyclin accumulation/degradation regulates CDK activity and how checkpoint or p53 mutations lead to cancer or apoptosis failure.

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 (Mitosis in Onion Root Tips & Lectin Signaling): Root tip squash stained with aceto-orcein/toluidine blue to compare mitotic index (% dividing cells = [cells in PMAT / total cells counted] × 100) between control meristem and phytohemagglutinin (lectin) or caffeine-treated roots. Chi-square (χ²) tests whether observed mitotic vs. interphase counts differ significantly from control. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Mitotic Index = (N_mitosis / N_total) × 100. DNA Content (C) & Ploidy (n) tracking in diploid (2n) somatic cells: G₁ = 2n, 2C (unreplicated chromosomes); S phase = DNA synthesis (2n, 2C → 4C); G₂/Prophase/Metaphase = 2n, 4C (sister chromatids joined); Anaphase = transient 4n, 4C (chromatids separate into individual chromosomes); Post-Cytokinesis = 2n, 2C. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Signaling Disruption Case Study: Vibrio cholerae secretes Cholera Toxin, which enzymatically modifies the G_α subunit of intestinal GPCRs so it cannot hydrolyze GTP → GDP. Locked in its active GTP-bound state, G_α continuously activates adenylyl cyclase, causing runaway cAMP accumulation, constitutive PKA activation, CFTR Cl⁻/H₂O efflux, and life-threatening secretory diarrhea. In cell cycle control, loss-of-function mutations in both alleles of TP53 ('guardian of the genome') prevent p21-mediated G₁/S arrest and apoptosis of DNA-damaged cells, driving oncogenic proliferation.
