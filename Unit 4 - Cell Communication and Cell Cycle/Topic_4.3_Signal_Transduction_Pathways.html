Cellular Responses to Signal Transduction 3 Major Outcomes
  • Depending on the cell type, receptor, and intracellular relay proteins present, signal transduction pathways culminate in 3 major functional outcomes:
  • 1. Gene Expression Changes (Nuclear Response — Slower, Long-Term):
    • Signaling cascades activate or inhibit specific transcription factors in the nucleus.
    • Turns transcription of target genes ON or OFF → alters mRNA and protein synthesis → changes cellular phenotype or triggers cell division/differentiation.
    • Example: Steroid hormones (cortisol, estrogen) or growth factors activating genes for cyclins.
  • 2. Direct Changes in Cell Function / Metabolism (Cytoplasmic Response — Fast):
    • Signals directly modify pre-existing cytoplasmic enzymes, membrane transport proteins, ion channels, or cytoskeleton filaments without altering DNA transcription.
    • Examples: Insulin triggers rapid exocytic insertion of GLUT4 glucose transporters into the plasma membrane to increase glucose uptake; Epinephrine activates glycogen phosphorylase to rapidly release glucose-1-phosphate.
  • 3. Apoptosis (Programmed Cell Death):
    • External death ligands or internal DNA/mitochondrial damage signals (cytochrome c release) activate a cascade of caspase proteases and nucleases that neatly dismantle the cell inside membrane blebs without causing inflammation.
    • Biological Roles: Embryonic development (removing webbing between human fingers/toes; tadpole tail resorption), eliminating virus-infected or DNA-damaged cells, and preventing cancer.
Common Mistake: Not all signaling pathways affect DNA or gene expression! Many pathways act exclusively in the cytoplasm (modifying existing enzymes or transporters like GLUT4) for rapid metabolic responses within seconds.

How Mutations Alter Signal Transduction Pathways Structure → Function
  • Mutations that alter the 3D structure of any protein in a pathway disrupt normal cellular responses:
  • 1. Receptor Mutations:
    • Extracellular Binding Domain Mutation: Ligand cannot bind → pathway remains permanently OFF even when hormone is present (e.g., androgen insensitivity or nephrogenic diabetes insipidus).
    • Intracellular Domain Loss-of-Function: Ligand binds outside, but signal cannot be relayed inside.
    • Constitutive Activation (Always "ON"): Mutant receptor fires continuously without any ligand bound → drives uncontrolled cell division (cancer, e.g., mutant HER2/EGF receptors).
  • 2. Second Messenger Disruptions (cAMP, Ca²⁺):
    • Inactivating adenylyl cyclase → no cAMP produced → entire downstream cascade fails.
    • Blocking cAMP breakdown (inhibiting phosphodiesterase) → prolongs and over-amplifies signaling.
  • 3. Protein Kinase / Phosphatase Mutations:
    • Inactive Kinase: Halts relay at that step; no downstream phosphorylation occurs.
    • Hyperactive / Constitutive Kinase (e.g., mutant Ras or B-Raf): Continuously phosphorylates downstream targets even without upstream signal → linked to uncontrolled mitosis / cancer.
  • 4. Transcription Factor Mutations:
    • Even if the receptor, second messengers, and kinases all work normally, a mutated transcription factor that cannot bind DNA causes the final gene expression response to fail (e.g., mutant p53 failing to activate cell-cycle arrest genes).
Chemical Modulation: Agonists, Antagonists & Toxins Pharmacology & Pathogens
Chemical Type Mechanism of Action High-Yield Examples
Agonists
(Activators)		 Mimic the natural ligand; bind to the receptor active site and induce the same conformational change to activate the pathway. Albuterol (mimics epinephrine at lung β₂ receptors to dilate airways); morphine/opioids (endorphin agonists); synthetic birth control hormones.
Antagonists
(Blockers)		 Bind to the receptor without activating it; competitively block the natural ligand from binding → inhibits pathway. Beta-blockers (block adrenaline/epinephrine receptors to lower heart rate & BP); Narcan (naloxone); antihistamines.
Cascade Modulators & Toxins Target intracellular G-proteins, adenylyl cyclase, phosphodiesterase (PDE), or kinases to block or lock signaling ON. • Caffeine: Inhibits PDE → prevents cAMP breakdown → prolongs wakefulness signal.
• Cholera Toxin: Modifies G-protein so it cannot hydrolyze GTP → locks adenylyl cyclase ON → massive cAMP → Cl⁻ & water efflux (severe diarrhea).
Agonist = Activator (mimics ligand & turns receptor ON)
Antagonist = Blocker (occupies receptor & prevents natural ligand from activating it)

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 (Mitosis in Onion Root Tips & Lectin Signaling): Root tip squash stained with aceto-orcein/toluidine blue to compare mitotic index (% dividing cells = [cells in PMAT / total cells counted] × 100) between control meristem and phytohemagglutinin (lectin) or caffeine-treated roots. Chi-square (χ²) tests whether observed mitotic vs. interphase counts differ significantly from control. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Mitotic Index = (N_mitosis / N_total) × 100. DNA Content (C) & Ploidy (n) tracking in diploid (2n) somatic cells: G₁ = 2n, 2C (unreplicated chromosomes); S phase = DNA synthesis (2n, 2C → 4C); G₂/Prophase/Metaphase = 2n, 4C (sister chromatids joined); Anaphase = transient 4n, 4C (chromatids separate into individual chromosomes); Post-Cytokinesis = 2n, 2C. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Signaling Disruption Case Study: Vibrio cholerae secretes Cholera Toxin, which enzymatically modifies the G_α subunit of intestinal GPCRs so it cannot hydrolyze GTP → GDP. Locked in its active GTP-bound state, G_α continuously activates adenylyl cyclase, causing runaway cAMP accumulation, constitutive PKA activation, CFTR Cl⁻/H₂O efflux, and life-threatening secretory diarrhea. In cell cycle control, loss-of-function mutations in both alleles of TP53 ('guardian of the genome') prevent p21-mediated G₁/S arrest and apoptosis of DNA-damaged cells, driving oncogenic proliferation.
