Cell Cycle Overview & Interphase (G₁, S, G₂) ~90% of Cell Cycle
  • Purpose of the Eukaryotic Cell Cycle: Highly regulated sequence of cell growth, DNA replication, and division that produces two genetically identical diploid (2n) daughter cells for organismal growth, tissue repair/regeneration, and asexual reproduction.
  • Interphase (~90% of Cycle Duration — Longest & Most Metabolically Active Stage):
    • G₁ Phase (First Gap): Cell is highly metabolically active; grows in volume, synthesizes proteins/RNAs, and duplicates cytoplasmic organelles (mitochondria, ER, ribosomes).
    • S Phase (Synthesis): DNA replication occurs! DNA remains loosely packed as chromatin. Each replicated chromosome now consists of two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere by cohesin proteins.
    • G₂ Phase (Second Gap): Final preparation for mitosis — rapid synthesis of microtubules/spindle proteins, high ATP production, and centrosome duplication completes.
  • G₀ Phase (Non-Dividing / Quiescent State):
    • Cells exit the active cell cycle at G₁ and do not divide, while remaining fully alive and metabolically active performing specialized physiological functions.
    • Permanent G₀: Mature human neurons and skeletal/cardiac muscle cells never divide again.
    • Temporary G₀: Liver cells (hepatocytes) and lymphocytes rest in G₀ but can re-enter G₁ when stimulated by external growth factors (e.g., after liver injury).
3 High-Frequency Exam Traps on Interphase & G₀:
1. Interphase is NOT a "resting phase" — the cell is furiously synthesizing DNA, RNA, proteins, and organelles!
2. G₀ does NOT mean dead or metabolically inactive — neurons in G₀ consume massive ATP firing action potentials!
3. Chromosome number (2n = 46) does NOT double in S phase! Only DNA mass/content (2C → 4C) and chromatid count (46 → 92) double because sister chromatids sharing 1 centromere still count as 1 chromosome!

Mitosis (M Phase: PMAT) & Cytokinesis Equal Genome Division
  • 1. Prophase (& Prometaphase):
    • Loosely coiled chromatin condenses into discrete, visible chromosomes (each made of 2 sister chromatids joined at the centromere).
    • Nucleolus disappears and nuclear envelope fragments.
    • Mitotic spindle (microtubules) forms as centrosomes migrate to opposite poles; spindle fibers attach to kinetochore protein plaques on centromeres.
  • 2. Metaphase:
    • Kinetochore microtubules tug chromosomes into a single-file line along the cell equator (the metaphase plate).
    • M-checkpoint verifies every kinetochore is attached to opposite poles before proceeding.
  • 3. Anaphase (Shortest Mitotic Phase):
    • Separase enzyme cleaves cohesin proteins holding sister chromatids together → sister chromatids separate.
    • Kinetochore microtubules depolymerize (shorten) to pull daughter chromosomes to opposite poles, while non-kinetochore microtubules elongate the cell.
  • 4. Telophase:
    • Two complete sets of chromosomes reach opposite poles and decondense back into diffuse chromatin.
    • Two nuclear envelopes reform around the daughter nuclei; nucleoli reappear; mitotic spindle depolymerizes.
  • 5. Cytokinesis (Division of the Cytoplasm — Overlaps Late Telophase):
    • Animal Cells (Cleavage Furrow): A contractile ring of actin microfilaments and myosin motor proteins pinches the plasma membrane inward from the outside until the cell is cleaved in two.
    • Plant Cells (Cell Plate): Because plants have a rigid cell wall, Golgi-derived vesicles carrying cell-wall polysaccharides coalesce at the midline to build a cell plate from the inside out, fusing with the plasma membrane to form a new cell wall.
Terminology Precision in Anaphase: The exact moment sister chromatids separate at the centromere in Anaphase, each chromatid is immediately considered a full, independent individual chromosome!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 (Mitosis in Onion Root Tips & Lectin Signaling): Root tip squash stained with aceto-orcein/toluidine blue to compare mitotic index (% dividing cells = [cells in PMAT / total cells counted] × 100) between control meristem and phytohemagglutinin (lectin) or caffeine-treated roots. Chi-square (χ²) tests whether observed mitotic vs. interphase counts differ significantly from control. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Mitotic Index = (N_mitosis / N_total) × 100. DNA Content (C) & Ploidy (n) tracking in diploid (2n) somatic cells: G₁ = 2n, 2C (unreplicated chromosomes); S phase = DNA synthesis (2n, 2C → 4C); G₂/Prophase/Metaphase = 2n, 4C (sister chromatids joined); Anaphase = transient 4n, 4C (chromatids separate into individual chromosomes); Post-Cytokinesis = 2n, 2C. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Signaling Disruption Case Study: Vibrio cholerae secretes Cholera Toxin, which enzymatically modifies the G_α subunit of intestinal GPCRs so it cannot hydrolyze GTP → GDP. Locked in its active GTP-bound state, G_α continuously activates adenylyl cyclase, causing runaway cAMP accumulation, constitutive PKA activation, CFTR Cl⁻/H₂O efflux, and life-threatening secretory diarrhea. In cell cycle control, loss-of-function mutations in both alleles of TP53 ('guardian of the genome') prevent p21-mediated G₁/S arrest and apoptosis of DNA-damaged cells, driving oncogenic proliferation.
