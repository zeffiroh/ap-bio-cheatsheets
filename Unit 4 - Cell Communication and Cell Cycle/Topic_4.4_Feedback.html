Homeostasis & Negative Feedback Mechanisms Stabilizes System
  • Homeostasis: The active maintenance of stable, relatively constant internal physiological conditions (around a target set point) despite fluctuations in the external environment.
  • Key Regulated Variables: Body temperature (~37°C), blood glucose (~90 mg/dL), blood pH (~7.4), blood osmolarity/water balance, and blood calcium.
  • Negative Feedback (Most Common in Biology):
    • Core Principle: Counteracts or reduces the initial stimulus, opposing any deviation from the normal range and returning the system back to its set point.
    • Loop Sequence: Deviation from Set Point → Sensor/Receptor Detects Change → Control Center → Effector Response Reverses Change → Return to Homeostasis.
    • Operates at molecular (allosteric feedback inhibition of enzymes, trp operon repression), cellular, and organismal levels.
3 High-Yield Negative Feedback Loops on the AP Exam:
  • 1. Blood Glucose Regulation (Insulin vs. Glucagon):
    • High Blood Glucose (after meal): Pancreatic β-cells secrete Insulin → liver & skeletal muscle take up glucose (via GLUT4) and store it as glycogen → blood glucose decreases back to set point.
    • Low Blood Glucose (fasting): Pancreatic α-cells secrete Glucagon → liver breaks down glycogen (glycogenolysis) and releases glucose into blood → blood glucose increases back to set point.
  • 2. Thermoregulation: High body temp → hypothalamus triggers sweating (evaporative cooling) & vasodilation → body temp decreases; Low temp → shivering & vasoconstriction → body temp increases.
  • 3. Osmoregulation (Water Balance & ADH): Dehydration (high blood osmolarity) → posterior pituitary releases ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone / Vasopressin) → inserts aquaporin channels into kidney collecting ducts → increases water reabsorption into blood → lowers osmolarity back to normal.
Negative Feedback = Stabilizes System (like a home thermostat: too hot → AC turns on → cools back down to set point).
Clinical Disruption: Type 1 vs. Type 2 Diabetes Broken Feedback Loop
Disorder Molecular Defect in Feedback Loop Treatment
Type 1 Diabetes Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells → no insulin ligand produced (signal is missing). Insulin injections / pump (replaces missing hormone ligand).
Type 2 Diabetes Insulin resistance: Target cells have defective insulin receptors or downstream RTK cascade (signal is ignored despite high insulin). Diet, exercise, drugs that restore receptor sensitivity.
Positive Feedback Mechanisms Amplifies to Endpoint
  • Core Principle: Amplifies or reinforces the initial stimulus, pushing the system further away from its starting state in an accelerating cycle until a defined physiological endpoint / completion event is reached.
  • Loop Sequence: Initial Stimulus → Response → Amplifies Stimulus → Stronger Response → Continues Until Endpoint Stops Loop.
  • Note: Positive feedback does NOT maintain day-to-day homeostasis around a set point; instead, it drives rapid, all-or-nothing biological processes that must finish quickly.
4 Classic AP Biology Positive Feedback Examples:
  • 1. Childbirth (Parturition — Oxytocin Loop): Baby's head pushes against cervix → nerve impulse to brain → pituitary secretes oxytocin → stimulates stronger uterine smooth muscle contractions → pushes baby harder against cervix → more oxytocin released → cycle accelerates until delivery of the baby ends cervical stretch!
  • 2. Fruit Ripening (Ethylene Gas): Ripening fruit cells release gaseous plant hormone ethylene (C₂H₄) → triggers neighboring fruit cells to ripen and synthesize even more ethylene ("one bad apple spoils the barrel").
  • 3. Lactation (Milk Letdown): Infant suckling stimulates oxytocin & prolactin release → milk ejection → encourages continued suckling.
  • 4. Blood Clotting Cascade: Activated platelets at a wound site release clotting signals that attract and activate more platelets until a fibrin clot seals the vessel.
Positive Feedback = Amplifies Change Until Endpoint!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 (Mitosis in Onion Root Tips & Lectin Signaling): Root tip squash stained with aceto-orcein/toluidine blue to compare mitotic index (% dividing cells = [cells in PMAT / total cells counted] × 100) between control meristem and phytohemagglutinin (lectin) or caffeine-treated roots. Chi-square (χ²) tests whether observed mitotic vs. interphase counts differ significantly from control. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Mitotic Index = (N_mitosis / N_total) × 100. DNA Content (C) & Ploidy (n) tracking in diploid (2n) somatic cells: G₁ = 2n, 2C (unreplicated chromosomes); S phase = DNA synthesis (2n, 2C → 4C); G₂/Prophase/Metaphase = 2n, 4C (sister chromatids joined); Anaphase = transient 4n, 4C (chromatids separate into individual chromosomes); Post-Cytokinesis = 2n, 2C. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Signaling Disruption Case Study: Vibrio cholerae secretes Cholera Toxin, which enzymatically modifies the G_α subunit of intestinal GPCRs so it cannot hydrolyze GTP → GDP. Locked in its active GTP-bound state, G_α continuously activates adenylyl cyclase, causing runaway cAMP accumulation, constitutive PKA activation, CFTR Cl⁻/H₂O efflux, and life-threatening secretory diarrhea. In cell cycle control, loss-of-function mutations in both alleles of TP53 ('guardian of the genome') prevent p21-mediated G₁/S arrest and apoptosis of DNA-damaged cells, driving oncogenic proliferation.
