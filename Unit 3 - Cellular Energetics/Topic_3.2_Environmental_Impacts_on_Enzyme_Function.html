Structure → Function & Enzyme Denaturation 3D Folding Integrity
  • Structure → Function Relationship: Enzyme catalytic activity depends strictly on its native 3D folding (secondary 2°, tertiary 3°, and quaternary 4° structure), which is stabilized by weak noncovalent interactions:
    • Hydrogen bonds (backbone & polar R-groups)
    • Ionic interactions / salt bridges (charged acidic & basic R-groups)
    • Hydrophobic interactions (nonpolar core) + covalent disulfide bridges (–S–S–)
  • Denaturation: Loss of native 3D protein conformation caused by extreme temperature, pH, salinity (ion concentration), or chemical denaturants → active site is distorted → substrate can no longer bind → loss of catalytic activity.
  • Reversible vs. Irreversible Denaturation:
    • Reversible (Renaturation): Mild environmental shift; once optimal conditions are restored, the polypeptide spontaneously refolds into its active shape.
    • Irreversible: Extreme heat or pH causes permanent aggregation/precipitation (e.g., boiling an egg white albumin).
Denaturation = enzyme unfolds → active site loses shape → no fit for substrate!
Primary (1°) structure is NOT broken during denaturation! Covalent peptide bonds remain intact (hydrolysis requires protease enzymes or harsh acid boiling); only weak 2°/3°/4° folding bonds are disrupted.
Temperature & pH Effects on Reaction Rate Asymmetric vs Bell Curves
  • Temperature Effects (Asymmetric Curve):
    • Below Optimum (↑ Temp → ↑ Rate): Higher thermal kinetic energy increases molecular velocity and frequency of successful enzyme-substrate collisions.
    • Above Optimum (Rapid Drop): Excessive thermal vibration breaks weak H-bonds and hydrophobic interactions → thermal denaturation → steep plunge in activity.
  • pH Effects (Symmetric Bell Curves):
    • Each enzyme has an optimal pH matching its cellular compartment.
    • Shifting [H⁺] protonates or deprotonates acidic (-COO⁻) and basic (-NH₃⁺) R-groups → alters side-chain charges → disrupts ionic bonds and active-site substrate binding.
Compartmental pH Specialization: Pepsin (stomach protease) has an acidic optimum of pH ~2; Trypsin (small intestine protease) has an alkaline optimum of pH ~8; Lysosomal hydrolases work at pH ~4.5–5 (inactive if leaked into pH 7.2 cytosol!).
Substrate Concentration, Saturation (Vₘₐₓ) & Kₘ Michaelis-Menten Kinetics
  • Substrate Concentration ([S]): At low [S], empty active sites are abundant → increasing [S] linearly increases reaction velocity (V₀).
  • Saturation & Vₘₐₓ: At high [S], the curve plateaus at maximum velocity (Vₘₐₓ) because 100% of enzyme active sites are saturated with substrate. The only way to increase Vₘₐₓ is to add more enzyme (↑ [E]).
  • Michaelis Constant (Kₘ — Affinity Inverse):
    • Defined as the substrate concentration [S] at exactly ½ Vₘₐₓ.
    • Low Kₘ = High substrate affinity (enzyme reaches ½ Vₘₐₓ at very low [S]; binds substrate tightly).
    • High Kₘ = Low substrate affinity (requires high [S] to reach ½ Vₘₐₓ).
Plateau on a [S] vs. Rate graph = All active sites are occupied!
Remember: Low Kₘ = binds substrate easily (high affinity).

Inhibition Type Binding Site & Mechanism Effect on Vₘₐₓ & Kₘ Overcome by ↑ [S]?
Competitive Active site ("fake key blocks lock"); mimics substrate shape. Vₘₐₓ unchanged;
↑ Kₘ (lower apparent affinity)		 YES — flooded [S] outcompetes inhibitor.
Noncompetitive (Allosteric) Allosteric site; changes enzyme 3D shape so active site cannot catalyze. ↓ Vₘₐₓ (fewer functional enzymes); Kₘ unchanged NO — more substrate does NOT fix warped active site.
  • Product / Feedback Inhibition (Negative Feedback): High concentration of a pathway's end product binds allosterically to the first committed enzyme in the pathway, shutting down synthesis to prevent waste of energy and raw materials.
FRQ Golden Rule: Always connect the full causal chain:
Environmental Change → Disrupts Weak Bonds (2°/3°) → Alters Active Site 3D Shape → ↓ ES Complex Formation → ↓ Reaction Rate!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 5, 6 & 13 (Enzyme Catalysis, Photosynthesis Leaf Disks & Respirometry): Enzyme assay (catalase/peroxidase spectrophotometry), Floating Leaf Disk Assay (ET₅₀ = time for 50% of disks to float in NaHCO₃), and KOH Respirometry (KOH absorbs CO₂ gas as a solid precipitate so volume decrease directly measures O₂ consumption). Negative Control: boiled enzyme/boiled seeds or glass beads (corrects for ambient temp/pressure changes). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Reaction Rate = ΔProduct / ΔTime (initial linear slope V₀). Michaelis-Menten Kinetics: Competitive inhibitors increase apparent K_m without changing V_max; Noncompetitive/allosteric inhibitors decrease V_max without changing K_m. Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG < 0 (exergonic/spontaneous, coupled via ATP → ADP + P_i, ΔG°' = −7.3 kcal/mol) vs. ΔG > 0 (endergonic). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Bioenergetic Disruption Case Study: Cyanide (CN⁻) and Carbon Monoxide (CO) bind irreversibly to Complex IV (Cytochrome c oxidase) of the mitochondrial ETC, blocking electron transfer to O₂, halting proton pumping, collapsing the H⁺ gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, and arresting ATP Synthase oxidative phosphorylation. Conversely, 2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP) and thermogenin (UCP1) make the inner membrane permeable to H⁺, uncoupling electron transport from ATP synthesis and dissipating proton-motive force as heat.
