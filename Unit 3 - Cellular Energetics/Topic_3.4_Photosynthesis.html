Photosynthesis Overview & Chloroplast Anatomy Light → Chemical Energy
  • Net Endergonic Redox Equation:
    6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ (ΔG = +686 kcal/mol)
    • H₂O is oxidized (loses e⁻/H⁺) to form O₂ gas (proven by ¹⁸O isotope tracing!).
    • CO₂ is reduced (gains e⁻/H⁺) to synthesize G3P / carbohydrates.
  • Evolutionary Origin (Endosymbiosis): Photosynthetic pathways evolved in prokaryotic cyanobacteria, which were engulfed by ancestral eukaryotes to become chloroplasts.
  • Chloroplast Compartmentalization:
    • Thylakoids & Grana (Stacks): Folded internal membranes containing chlorophyll a/b pigments, Photosystems II & I, ETC proteins (plastoquinone, cytochrome b₆f), and ATP synthase. Site of the Light-Dependent Reactions.
    • Thylakoid Lumen (Space): Tiny enclosed interior volume where H⁺ ions accumulate (low pH ~5) to rapidly build a steep proton-motive force.
    • Stroma: Fluid-filled space outside the thylakoids (higher pH ~8); contains RuBisCO and enzymes for the Calvin Cycle.
Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid Membrane) PSII (P680) → PSI (P700)
  • 1. Photoexcitation at Photosystem II (PSII — P680 acts FIRST): Photons absorbed by antenna pigments excite electrons in reaction-center chlorophyll P680 to a primary electron acceptor.
  • 2. Photolysis (Water Splitting): An oxygen-evolving enzyme splits water inside the thylakoid lumen (2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ + O₂) to replace electrons lost from PSII.
  • 3. Linear Electron Transport Chain (ETC) & H⁺ Pumping: Excited electrons pass down from PSII through plastoquinone (Pq) → Cytochrome Complex → plastocyanin (Pc) to PSI. Energy released actively pumps H⁺ from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen.
  • 4. Chemiosmosis (Photophosphorylation): High [H⁺] inside the thylakoid lumen flows down its electrochemical gradient back into the stroma through ATP Synthase, driving ADP + Pᵢ → ATP.
  • 5. Photosystem I (PSI — P700 acts SECOND) & NADPH Formation: Light re-excites electrons at PSI; electrons pass via ferredoxin (Fd) to NADP⁺ Reductase, reducing NADP⁺ + 2e⁻ + H⁺ → NADPH (the final electron acceptor!).
High-Yield Exam Trap: In photosynthesis, excited electrons end up in NADPH (final electron acceptor is NADP⁺), NOT oxygen! Water (H₂O) is the initial electron donor for PSII.
Linear (Noncyclic) vs. Cyclic Photophosphorylation Balancing ATP : NADPH
Feature Noncyclic (Linear) Flow Cyclic Electron Flow
Photosystems Used Uses BOTH PSII (P680) and PSI (P700) Uses PSI (P700) ONLY
Electron Path H₂O → PSII → ETC → PSI → NADP⁺ (not recycled) Excited e⁻ from PSI cycle back through Cyt b₆f ETC and return to PSI

Not Produced — NO NADPH and NO O₂ (no water splitting!)
Why Cyclic Flow Exists: The Calvin Cycle consumes more ATP (9) than NADPH (6) per G3P made; cyclic photophosphorylation generates extra ATP to make up this stoichiometric deficit.
The Calvin Cycle: Carbon Fixation in the Stroma 3 Phases → G3P Sugar
  • Overview: Occurs in the stroma; uses CO₂ + ATP + NADPH (from the light reactions) to synthesize the 3-carbon sugar G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate), which is used to build glucose, sucrose, starch, and cellulose.
  • Phase 1 — Carbon Fixation: The enzyme RuBisCO catalyzes the attachment of inorganic CO₂ (1C) to the 5-carbon starting acceptor RuBP (ribulose bisphosphate, 5C) → forms an unstable 6C intermediate that immediately splits into two molecules of 3-PGA (3C).
  • Phase 2 — Reduction: ATP phosphorylates intermediates and NADPH donates high-energy electrons (oxidized back to ADP + Pᵢ and NADP⁺, which return to the thylakoids!) to reduce 3-PGA into G3P (3C). For every 3 CO₂ fixed, 6 G3P are formed, and 1 net G3P exits the cycle.
  • Phase 3 — Regeneration of RuBP: The remaining 5 G3P molecules (15 carbons total) are rearranged using additional ATP to regenerate 3 RuBP (15 carbons) so the cycle can continue fixing CO₂.
Classic FRQ Trap — What Happens When Light Is Turned Off?
The Calvin Cycle requires light indirectly! Without light reactions running, ATP and NADPH production stops immediately → the Reduction & Regeneration phases halt → 3-PGA accumulates while G3P and RuBP levels drop (or if CO₂ is removed in light, RuBP accumulates!).

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 5, 6 & 13 (Enzyme Catalysis, Photosynthesis Leaf Disks & Respirometry): Enzyme assay (catalase/peroxidase spectrophotometry), Floating Leaf Disk Assay (ET₅₀ = time for 50% of disks to float in NaHCO₃), and KOH Respirometry (KOH absorbs CO₂ gas as a solid precipitate so volume decrease directly measures O₂ consumption). Negative Control: boiled enzyme/boiled seeds or glass beads (corrects for ambient temp/pressure changes). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Reaction Rate = ΔProduct / ΔTime (initial linear slope V₀). Michaelis-Menten Kinetics: Competitive inhibitors increase apparent K_m without changing V_max; Noncompetitive/allosteric inhibitors decrease V_max without changing K_m. Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG < 0 (exergonic/spontaneous, coupled via ATP → ADP + P_i, ΔG°' = −7.3 kcal/mol) vs. ΔG > 0 (endergonic). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Bioenergetic Disruption Case Study: Cyanide (CN⁻) and Carbon Monoxide (CO) bind irreversibly to Complex IV (Cytochrome c oxidase) of the mitochondrial ETC, blocking electron transfer to O₂, halting proton pumping, collapsing the H⁺ gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, and arresting ATP Synthase oxidative phosphorylation. Conversely, 2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP) and thermogenin (UCP1) make the inner membrane permeable to H⁺, uncoupling electron transport from ATP synthesis and dissipating proton-motive force as heat.
