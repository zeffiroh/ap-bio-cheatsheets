Overview, Macromolecule Fuels & Mitochondria Exergonic Redox
  • Overall Exergonic Equation:
    C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ~30–32 ATP + Heat (ΔG = −686 kcal/mol)
    • Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is oxidized to 6 CO₂; Oxygen (O₂) is reduced to 6 H₂O.
  • Macromolecules as Energy Sources:
    • Carbohydrates: Primary, immediate fuel source (~4 kcal/g); glucose enters glycolysis directly.
    • Lipids (Fats): Long-term energy reserve; abundant reduced C–H bonds yield >2× more ATP per gram (~9 kcal/g) via beta-oxidation into Acetyl-CoA.
    • Proteins: Used only during prolonged starvation after deamination (removing -NH₂ as urea).
  • Mitochondrial Double-Membrane Structure → Function:
    • Outer Membrane: Smooth boundary separating mitochondrion from cytosol.
    • Inner Membrane (Cristae): Highly folded infoldings (cristae) that dramatically increase surface area to volume ratio to embed thousands of copies of ETC protein complexes and ATP Synthase.
    • Intermembrane Space: Narrow space between membranes where H⁺ ions are pumped (low pH / acidic).
    • Mitochondrial Matrix: Innermost fluid compartment (higher pH); site of Pyruvate Oxidation and the Krebs Cycle.
Stages 1–3: Glycolysis, Link Reaction & Krebs Cycle Harvesting NADH & FADH₂
  • Stage 1 — Glycolysis (Cytosol | Anaerobic — No O₂ Needed):
    • Splits 1 Glucose (6C) → 2 Pyruvate (3C) in a 10-step enzyme pathway conserved across all domains of life.
    • Energy Investment (2 ATP used) vs. Payoff (4 ATP made): Yields 2 Net ATP (via substrate-level phosphorylation) + 2 NADH (from 2 NAD⁺) + 0 CO₂.
  • Stage 2 — Pyruvate Oxidation / Link Reaction (Mitochondrial Matrix):
    • Each Pyruvate (3C) is actively transported into the matrix, loses a carboxyl group as CO₂, reduces NAD⁺ → NADH, and attaches to Coenzyme A to form Acetyl-CoA (2C).
    • Yield per Glucose (2 Pyruvates): 2 Acetyl-CoA + 2 CO₂ + 2 NADH (0 ATP).
  • Stage 3 — Krebs / Citric Acid Cycle (Mitochondrial Matrix):
    • Completes the oxidation of organic carbon: Acetyl-CoA (2C) joins oxaloacetate (4C) to form citrate (6C), which is oxidized stepwise back to 4C.
    • Yield per Glucose (2 turns): 4 CO₂ + 2 ATP (GTP) + 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂.
    • Main Purpose: Generate loaded electron carriers (NADH & FADH₂) to feed the ETC!
Two Classic Exam Mistakes:
1. Glycolysis makes 4 Gross ATP, but only 2 NET ATP per glucose.
2. Most ATP is NOT made in the Krebs cycle (only 2 ATP!) — ~90% of ATP (~26–28 ATP) is made in Oxidative Phosphorylation.
Stage 4: Oxidative Phosphorylation (ETC & Chemiosmosis) Inner Membrane Cristae
  • Electron Transport Chain (ETC — Complexes I–IV):
    • NADH (at Complex I) and FADH₂ (at Complex II) are oxidized, donating high-energy electrons harvested from food.
    • Electrons step down a chain of increasingly electronegative redox carriers, releasing free energy used by Complexes I, III, and IV to actively pump H⁺ from the matrix into the intermembrane space.
    • Final Electron Acceptor = Oxygen (O₂): Highly electronegative O₂ pulls electrons at the bottom of the chain and combines with matrix protons to form water (½ O₂ + 2e⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O). Without O₂, the entire ETC backs up and halts!
  • Proton Gradient & Chemiosmosis (ATP Synthase):
    • Intermembrane Space: High [H⁺] → Low pH (more acidic).
    • Mitochondrial Matrix: Low [H⁺] → Higher pH (more basic).
    • H⁺ ions flow back down their electrochemical gradient into the matrix through the rotary ATP Synthase turbine, driving oxidative phosphorylation (ADP + Pᵢ → ~26–28 ATP).
    • Uncoupling (Thermogenesis): Uncoupler proteins (e.g., UCP1 / thermogenin in hibernating mammal brown fat) create H⁺ leak channels across the inner membrane → dissipates the proton gradient as body heat instead of making ATP!
Stage Summary Table & Anaerobic Fermentation Regenerating NAD⁺

1. Glycolysis Cytosol 1 Glucose (6C), 2 NAD⁺, 2 ATP 2 Pyruvate, 2 Net ATP, 2 NADH
2. Pyruvate Oxid. Matrix 2 Pyruvate (3C), 2 NAD⁺, CoA 2 Acetyl-CoA, 2 CO₂, 2 NADH
3. Krebs Cycle Matrix 2 Acetyl-CoA (2C), 6 NAD⁺, 2 FAD 4 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂
4. Ox. Phosphor. Inner Memb. 10 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 6 O₂ ~26–28 ATP, 6 H₂O, 10 NAD⁺, 2 FAD
  • Anaerobic Fermentation (Cytosol — No O₂ Required | Yield = 2 ATP):
    • Sole Purpose: Oxidize NADH → NAD⁺ by transferring electrons to pyruvate (or its derivative) so glycolysis does not run out of NAD⁺ and can continue making 2 ATP per glucose! Fermentation steps themselves produce 0 additional ATP.
    • Lactic Acid Fermentation: Pyruvate is directly reduced by NADH → Lactate (lactic acid, 3C) + NAD⁺ (no CO₂ released; occurs in human skeletal muscle & yogurt bacteria).
    • Alcohol Fermentation: Pyruvate → acetaldehyde + CO₂ → reduced by NADH to Ethanol (2C) + NAD⁺ (occurs in yeast & some bacteria).
AP Exam Scope Note: You do NOT need to memorize individual intermediate names of glycolysis/Krebs or specific enzyme/carrier names — focus on compartment locations, H⁺ gradient pH directions, inputs/outputs, and why O₂ or fermentation is required.

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 5, 6 & 13 (Enzyme Catalysis, Photosynthesis Leaf Disks & Respirometry): Enzyme assay (catalase/peroxidase spectrophotometry), Floating Leaf Disk Assay (ET₅₀ = time for 50% of disks to float in NaHCO₃), and KOH Respirometry (KOH absorbs CO₂ gas as a solid precipitate so volume decrease directly measures O₂ consumption). Negative Control: boiled enzyme/boiled seeds or glass beads (corrects for ambient temp/pressure changes). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Reaction Rate = ΔProduct / ΔTime (initial linear slope V₀). Michaelis-Menten Kinetics: Competitive inhibitors increase apparent K_m without changing V_max; Noncompetitive/allosteric inhibitors decrease V_max without changing K_m. Gibbs Free Energy: ΔG < 0 (exergonic/spontaneous, coupled via ATP → ADP + P_i, ΔG°' = −7.3 kcal/mol) vs. ΔG > 0 (endergonic). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Bioenergetic Disruption Case Study: Cyanide (CN⁻) and Carbon Monoxide (CO) bind irreversibly to Complex IV (Cytochrome c oxidase) of the mitochondrial ETC, blocking electron transfer to O₂, halting proton pumping, collapsing the H⁺ gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, and arresting ATP Synthase oxidative phosphorylation. Conversely, 2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP) and thermogenin (UCP1) make the inner membrane permeable to H⁺, uncoupling electron transport from ATP synthesis and dissipating proton-motive force as heat.
