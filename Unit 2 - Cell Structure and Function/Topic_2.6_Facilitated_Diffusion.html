Core Features of Facilitated Diffusion Passive + Protein
  • Definition: Spontaneous, passive transport of polar or charged substances across the lipid bilayer assisted by specific transmembrane transport proteins.
  • 4 Defining Characteristics:
    • 1. Passive (No ATP Required): Driven purely by the solute's own thermal motion down its concentration or electrochemical gradient ([High] → [Low]).
    • 2. Requires Integral Transport Proteins: Shield polar/charged solutes from the hydrophobic fatty acid core of the bilayer.
    • 3. Solute-Specific: Each channel or carrier protein has a specific binding site or selectivity filter (e.g., a K⁺ channel will not pass Na⁺; GLUT1 transports glucose, not lactose).
    • 4. Exhibits Saturation Kinetics (Vₘₐₓ): Unlike simple diffusion (which increases linearly forever with concentration), facilitated diffusion plateaus (saturates) when all transport proteins in the membrane are occupied!
Facilitated Diffusion = Protein-Mediated + Passive (0 ATP)! If a graph of transport rate vs. solute concentration curves and levels off at a plateau (Vₘₐₓ) WITHOUT using ATP, it is facilitated diffusion (carrier/channel saturation), NOT simple diffusion!
Channel Proteins vs. Carrier Proteins Tunnels vs. Revolving Doors
Feature Channel Proteins Carrier Proteins (Uniporters / Permeases)
Main Cargo Small charged ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻) & water (H₂O) Large polar uncharged solutes (e.g., glucose, amino acids, nucleosides)
Mechanism Forms a continuous hydrophilic pore/tunnel across the bilayer; ions/water flow through without binding tightly Solute binds specific site → protein undergoes conformational shape change → releases solute on opposite side
Transport Speed Extremely fast (~10⁷–10⁸ ions/sec per channel) Slower (~10²–10⁴ molecules/sec); limited by shape-change cycle
Classic Examples Aquaporins, voltage-gated Na⁺/K⁺ channels, CFTR Cl⁻ channel GLUT1 / GLUT4 glucose transporters, amino acid carriers
  • Ion Channels = Selective Hydrophilic Tunnels (ultra-fast flow of ions or water).
  • Carrier Proteins = Revolving Doors (bind large polar solute like glucose → change shape → release).
Diagnostic Decision Rule: Large + Polar molecule (Glucose / Amino Acid) + Moves High → Low + NO ATP = Facilitated Diffusion via a Carrier Protein!
Aquaporins: Specialized Water Channels Rapid Osmosis
  • Why Aquaporins Are Needed: Although water (H₂O) is small enough to slip slowly between phospholipid tails in limited amounts, cells that transport large volumes of water require aquaporins — specialized tetrameric integral channel proteins dedicated to water.
  • Function & Selectivity:
    • Allow up to 3 × 10⁹ water molecules per second to cross in single file via passive facilitated diffusion (osmosis) down the water potential gradient (Ψ), with zero ATP.
    • Narrow hydrophilic pore lined with positively charged side chains strictly blocks ions (including hydronium H₃O⁺ / H⁺) so membrane proton gradients are preserved!
Physiological Regulation of Aquaporins:
  • Kidney Collecting Duct Cells & ADH (Vasopressin): Pituitary hormone ADH triggers cyclic AMP signaling that inserts Aquaporin-2 (AQP2) vesicles into the apical membrane → dramatically increases water reabsorption into blood! (Mutations cause nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.)
  • Also abundant in red blood cells and plant root hair / tonoplast membranes.
Ion Channels: Leak vs. Gated Channels Regulated Ion Flow
  • Charged ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻) cannot cross the hydrophobic lipid core directly → require selective ion channels:
    • 1. Non-Gated (Leak) Channels: Always open (or flicker randomly); K⁺ leak channels allow slow K⁺ efflux that establishes the negative resting membrane potential.
    • 2. Gated Ion Channels: Open or close in response to a specific physiological stimulus:
      • Voltage-Gated Channels: Open/close in response to a change in membrane electrical potential (mV) (e.g., voltage-gated Na⁺ and K⁺ channels along neuron axons during an action potential).
      • Ligand-Gated (Chemically Gated) Channels: Open when a specific extracellular or intracellular signaling molecule (ligand/neurotransmitter) binds (e.g., acetylcholine [ACh] binding nicotinic Na⁺ receptors at the neuromuscular junction).
      • Mechanically Gated Channels: Open in response to physical stretch, pressure, or vibration (e.g., inner ear hair cells for hearing; touch receptors).
Cystic Fibrosis (CFTR Channel Mutation): Caused by a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR Cl⁻ channel protein. Defective Cl⁻ export prevents water from following by osmosis into airway mucus → results in thick, sticky mucus in lungs and pancreas.
Membrane Potential & Cellular Polarization Neurons & Muscle
  • Membrane Potential (Vₘ): Voltage (electrical potential difference) across the plasma membrane caused by an unequal distribution of positive and negative ions between the cytosol and extracellular fluid.
  • Polarized Membrane at Rest (≈ −70 mV in Neurons):
    • Extracellular fluid is rich in Na⁺ and Cl⁻ (net positive charge outside: +).
    • Cytosol is rich in K⁺ and large negatively charged organic anions (proteins, PO₄³⁻, nucleic acids) (net negative charge inside: −).
  • How Facilitated Diffusion Changes Membrane Potential:
    • Opening voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → rapid facilitated diffusion of Na⁺ INTO the cell → makes inside positive (depolarization, firing a nerve impulse or muscle contraction).
    • Opening voltage-gated K⁺ channels → facilitated diffusion of K⁺ OUT OF the cell → restores negative interior (repolarization).
Active vs. Passive Partnership in Neurons: The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump (active transport) establishes and maintains the Na⁺ and K⁺ gradients using ATP, while gated Na⁺ and K⁺ ion channels (facilitated diffusion) use those gradients to generate rapid electrical signals without ATP!

Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane. Impaired Cl⁻ efflux prevents osmotic water movement into mucus, producing thick, dehydrated mucus in lungs and pancreatic ducts. Ouabain/Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, collapsing the electrochemical gradient required for secondary active cotransport.
