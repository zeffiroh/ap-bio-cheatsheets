Primary vs. Secondary Active Transport (Cotransport) Energy Coupling
  • Role of Integral Membrane Proteins in Active Transport:
    • Integral carrier proteins (pumps & cotransporters) are highly specific for their target ions/solutes and undergo reversible conformational shape changes to move substances uphill against their concentration or electrochemical gradient ([Low] → [High]).
Feature Primary Active Transport Secondary Active Transport (Cotransport)
Direct Energy Source Direct ATP hydrolysis (ATP → ADP + Pᵢ); phosphate group directly phosphorylates the pump Potential energy stored in an electrochemical gradient (created earlier by a primary ATP pump; no direct ATP binding)
Mechanism Pumps ion(s) uphill (Low → High) to build a steep electrochemical gradient across the membrane Driving ion (Na⁺ or H⁺) diffuses downhill (High → Low) through cotransporter, pulling a second solute uphill (Low → High)
Classic Examples Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump (animal cells), H⁺ proton pump (plants, fungi, bacteria, lysosomes), Ca²⁺ ATPase (SERCA) Na⁺/Glucose Symporter (SGLT1) in gut/kidney; H⁺/Sucrose Cotransporter in plant phloem loading
  • Two Types of Secondary Active Cotransporters:
    • Symport (Symporter): Both the driving ion and the co-transported solute move in the SAME direction across the membrane (e.g., Na⁺ and glucose both enter intestinal epithelial cells via SGLT1).
    • Antiport (Antiporter / Exchanger): The driving ion moves downhill in one direction while the second ion/solute is driven uphill in the OPPOSITE direction (e.g., Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger in cardiac muscle: Na⁺ enters downhill while Ca²⁺ is exported uphill).
  • Primary Active Transport = Directly uses ATP to build an ion gradient.
  • Secondary Active Transport (Cotransport) = Indirectly uses ATP by harvesting the electrochemical gradient built by the primary pump! (If ATP production stops via cyanide/dinitrophenol, secondary active transport soon stops too as the gradient runs down!)
The Sodium-Potassium Pump (Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase) 3 Na⁺ Out / 2 K⁺ In
  • Classic Primary Active Electorgenic Pump of Animal Cells:
    • Stoichiometry per Cycle: Exports 3 Na⁺ OUT of the cell and imports 2 K⁺ INTO the cell at the cost of 1 ATP hydrolyzed.
    • Both ions move AGAINST their concentration gradients: Maintains high [Na⁺] outside (~145 mM out vs. 12 mM in) and high [K⁺] inside (~140 mM in vs. 4 mM out).
    • Electrogenic Effect: Because 3 positive charges leave for every 2 positive charges that enter (net export of +1 charge per cycle), it directly generates a net negative charge inside the cell!
  • 4 Step Conformational Cycle:
    • Step 1: Cytoplasmic 3 Na⁺ bind high-affinity sites on the inward-facing pump.
    • Step 2: ATP hydrolyzes (ATP → ADP + Pᵢ), covalently phosphorylating the pump (Aspartate residue).
    • Step 3: Phosphorylation triggers a conformational shape change to outward-facing → releases 3 Na⁺ outside and binds extracellular 2 K⁺.
    • Step 4: Binding of 2 K⁺ triggers dephosphorylation (release of Pᵢ) → pump returns to original shape and releases 2 K⁺ into the cytosol.

Electrochemical Gradients, Membrane Potential & H⁺ Pumps Bioenergetics
  • Electrochemical Gradients Store Potential Energy:
    • Combines the concentration gradient (chemical force) and the membrane voltage (electrical force).
    • Drives three essential cellular processes: (1) rapid ion flux in signaling, (2) secondary active cotransport of nutrients, and (3) chemiosmotic ATP synthesis.
  • Membrane Potential (Vₘ ≈ −70 mV in Animal Cells):
    • Established by the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump + open K⁺ leak channels.
    • Essential for action potentials (nerve impulses) in neurons and excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal/cardiac muscle cells.
  • Proton Gradients (H⁺ Pumps & Proton-Motive Force):
    • In Plants, Fungi & Bacteria: The Proton Pump (H⁺-ATPase) is the main electrogenic pump on the plasma membrane — uses ATP to pump H⁺ out of the cell, driving H⁺/sucrose cotransport (phloem loading) and root mineral (NO₃⁻) uptake.
    • In Lysosomes & Plant Vacuoles: V-type H⁺-ATPase pumps H⁺ inside to maintain an acidic lumen (pH ~4.8) for hydrolytic enzymes.
    • In Mitochondria & Chloroplasts (Chemiosmosis): Electron transport chains pump H⁺ across the inner mitochondrial membrane (into the intermembrane space) or across the thylakoid membrane (into the thylakoid lumen). This steep H⁺ gradient (proton-motive force) flows back down through ATP Synthase to power ADP + Pᵢ → ATP!
Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT) Medical Application: Cholera toxin causes fatal osmotic diarrhea by locking intestinal Cl⁻ channels open. Drinking plain water fails, but drinking a solution of both NaCl + Glucose activates the intestinal Na⁺/Glucose Symporter (SGLT1) → pulls Na⁺ and glucose into intestinal cells, lowering internal Ψs so water rapidly follows by osmosis (saving millions of lives)!
Cross-Unit Link (Units 2, 3, & 4):
  • Animal cells rely primarily on the Na⁺/K⁺ pump for plasma membrane gradients.
  • Plant, fungal, & bacterial cells rely primarily on the H⁺ proton pump for plasma membrane cotransport.
  • Mitochondria & Chloroplasts use ETC-driven H⁺ gradients to drive ATP synthase (facilitated diffusion of H⁺ coupled to phosphorylation!).

IV: Extracellular sucrose molarity (M); DV: % change in mass; Isotonic point: x-intercept where % ΔMass = 0% (no net water movement). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane. Impaired Cl⁻ efflux prevents osmotic water movement into mucus, producing thick, dehydrated mucus in lungs and pancreatic ducts. Ouabain/Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, collapsing the electrochemical gradient required for secondary active cotransport.
