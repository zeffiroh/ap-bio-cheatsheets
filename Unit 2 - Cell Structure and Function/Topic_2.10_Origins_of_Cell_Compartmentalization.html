Two Evolutionary Origins of Eukaryotic Organelles Infolding vs. Endosymbiosis
  • 1. Origin of the Endomembrane System (Autogenous Membrane Infolding):
    • The nuclear envelope, endoplasmic reticulum (ER), Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and vacuoles evolved from inward foldings (invaginations) of the ancestral archaeal host cell's own plasma membrane (~2.1 billion years ago).
    • Explains why endomembrane organelles have single lipid bilayers (or a contiguous double fold in the nuclear envelope) and why their internal lumen is topologically equivalent to the exterior of the cell.
  • 2. Origin of Mitochondria & Chloroplasts (Endosymbiotic Theory — Lynn Margulis):
    • Mitochondria and chloroplasts evolved from once free-living prokaryotes (bacteria) that were engulfed via phagocytosis by an ancestral host cell (an anaerobic archaeon/proto-eukaryote), escaped digestion, and established a mutually beneficial endosymbiosis.
    • Over evolutionary time, extensive horizontal gene transfer moved many endosymbiont genes into the host nucleus, making the host and organelle mutually interdependent (1 single organism).
1. Membrane Infolding → Nucleus & ER → 2. Engulf Aerobic α-Proteobacterium → Mitochondria (ALL Eukaryotes!) → 3. Engulf Cyanobacterium → Chloroplasts (Plants & Algae)
Serial Endosymbiosis Order (Why Mitochondria Came FIRST!):
  • Nearly ALL eukaryotes (animals, plants, fungi, protists) have mitochondria (or mitochondrial remnants), whereas only photosynthetic eukaryotes (plants & algae) have chloroplasts.
  • Therefore, the engulfment of the aerobic prokaryote (mitochondrion) occurred FIRST in the common ancestor of all eukaryotes; later, one lineage of mitochondrion-bearing eukaryotes engulfed a photosynthetic cyanobacterium (chloroplast)!

5 Key Lines of Evidence for Endosymbiosis High-Yield FRQ List

Mitochondria and chloroplasts share five diagnostic structural, genetic, and biochemical traits with modern prokaryotes (bacteria):

Line of Evidence In Mitochondria & Chloroplasts Why It Supports Bacterial Origin
1. Double Membrane Surrounded by two phospholipid bilayers (inner + outer membrane) Inner membrane = original bacterial plasma membrane (has bacterial cardiolipin & ETC); Outer membrane = derived from host's phagocytic vesicle!
2. Own Circular, Naked DNA Contain their own single, circular DNA molecule lacking histone proteins Matches bacterial chromosomes/plasmids (unlike linear, histone-wrapped nuclear eukaryotic chromosomes)
3. Own 70S Ribosomes Transcribe & translate their own proteins using internal 70S ribosomes Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes (sensitive to bacterial antibiotics like streptomycin/tetracycline), whereas eukaryotic cytosol has 80S ribosomes!
4. Independent Binary Fission Replicate inside the cell by splitting in two (binary fission) independently of mitosis Same asexual division mechanism (using FtsZ proteins) as free-living bacteria; if all mitochondria are removed, a cell cannot make new ones from scratch!
5. Size, Porins & Phylogeny ~1–5 μm size; outer membrane has bacterial porin proteins; rRNA sequences Mitochondrial rRNA groups phylogenetically with α-proteobacteria; chloroplast rRNA groups with cyanobacteria
FRQ Scoring Rule: Always state at least 3 distinct pieces of evidence when asked to justify Endosymbiotic Theory: (1) Double membrane, (2) Own circular naked DNA, (3) Own 70S ribosomes, and (4) Reproduce via binary fission!
Common Ribosome Mix-Up: Never confuse 70S ribosomes (found in prokaryotes, mitochondria, and chloroplasts) with 80S ribosomes (found in the eukaryotic cytosol and on Rough ER)!
Evolutionary Significance & Prokaryotic Microdomains Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes
  • Evolutionary Significance of Endosymbiosis:
    • Mitochondria provided massive aerobic ATP production (~30–32 ATP/glucose vs. 2 ATP from glycolysis) across vast internal cristae membranes.
    • Chloroplasts gave eukaryotic algae & plants autotrophic carbon fixation via oxygenic photosynthesis.
    • Result: Enabled the evolution of much larger, structurally complex eukaryotic cells and eventually multicellular organisms with specialized tissues.
  • Do Prokaryotes Have Compartmentalization? (Prokaryotic Microdomains):
    • Prokaryotes (Bacteria & Archaea) lack internal membrane-bound organelles, but they still organize metabolic pathways into specialized non-membrane regions / protein microcompartments:
    • 1. The Nucleoid Region: Non-membrane-bound region where the circular bacterial chromosome is condensed and organized by nucleoid-associated proteins.
    • 2. Carboxysomes (Bacterial Microcompartments): Polyhedral protein shells (NOT lipid membranes!) found in cyanobacteria and chemoautotrophs that encapsulate RuBisCO and carbonic anhydrase → concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO to boost Calvin cycle carbon fixation efficiency and block photorespiration!
    • 3. Infalded Plasma Membrane Regions (Thylakoid-like / Mesosomes): Cyanobacteria fold their own plasma membrane inward to house photosynthetic electron transport chains.
Prokaryotes = Open-Plan Workspace with Organized Zones: No internal lipid walls (no membrane-bound organelles), but organized functional zones like the nucleoid and protein-shelled carboxysomes!
Nucleoid ≠ Nucleus! A nucleus is a double-membrane-bound eukaryotic organelle; a nucleoid is simply the non-membrane-bound cytoplasmic region where prokaryotic circular DNA resides.

Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
