Phospholipid Bilayer: Amphipathic Self-Assembly Core Framework
  • Amphipathic Molecules: Phospholipids possess both a hydrophilic (water-loving, polar) region and a hydrophobic (water-fearing, nonpolar) region:
    • Hydrophilic Head: Glycerol + negatively charged phosphate group (-PO₄²⁻) + choline → faces outward toward aqueous extracellular fluid and inward toward aqueous cytosol.
    • Hydrophobic Tails: Two long nonpolar fatty acid hydrocarbon chains → point inward away from water, forming a nonpolar interior core held together by weak van der Waals / hydrophobic interactions.
  • Spontaneous Bilayer Formation: In aqueous environments, phospholipids spontaneously self-assemble into a bilayer (driven by entropy/hydrophobic exclusion of water), creating a stable, selectively permeable barrier separating internal vs. external environments.
The plasma membrane is DYNAMIC and FLUID, NOT rigid or static! Phospholipids and many proteins drift laterally (~2 μm/sec) within their leaflet because they are held by weak hydrophobic interactions, not covalent bonds.
Dynamic Membrane Flexibility: Enables white blood cells (leukocytes) to deform and squeeze through narrow capillary walls (diapedesis), self-seal after microneedle puncture, and form/fuse vesicles during endocytosis & exocytosis.

Glycoproteins & Glycolipids (Extracellular Surface) Cell Recognition
  • Found exclusively on the exterior (extracellular) face of the plasma membrane (forming the glycocalyx):
    • Glycoprotein = Membrane Protein + covalently bonded Carbohydrate chain (oligosaccharide).
    • Glycolipid = Phospholipid + covalently bonded Carbohydrate chain.
  • Key Biological Roles:
    • Cell-to-Cell Recognition & Immune Response: Distinguish "self" from foreign "non-self" cells (e.g., MHC — Major Histocompatibility Complex glycoproteins in vertebrate immunity; A, B, O blood group antigens on red blood cells).
    • Cell Signaling & Adhesion: Tissue sorting during embryonic development and intercellular communication.
Carbohydrates on the membrane = Cell ID Tags! Always oriented toward the outside (extracellular side) of the plasma membrane.
Membrane Proteins: Integral vs. Peripheral Mosaic of Functions
  • Membrane proteins can be hydrophilic, hydrophobic, or amphipathic depending on their amino acid R-groups:
Protein Class Location & R-Group Polarity Structural & Functional Roles
Integral (Transmembrane) Proteins Embedded through the lipid bilayer; nonpolar/hydrophobic R-groups (often α-helices) anchor in the fatty acid core, while polar/charged R-groups protrude into water on both sides Channels, carrier proteins, active pumps (Na⁺/K⁺ pump), signal transduction receptors (GPCRs, RTKs)
Peripheral Proteins Loosely bound to the hydrophilic surface of the membrane (often attached to integral proteins or polar lipid heads); rich in polar/charged R-groups Cytoskeletal anchoring, intracellular signaling cascades, enzymes, structural support
  • 6 Major Functions of Membrane Proteins (Mnemonic: "TRACIE"):
    • T — Transport: Hydrophilic channels, carriers, and ATP-driven pumps.
    • R — Receptors (Signal Transduction): Specific binding sites for hormones/neurotransmitters (ligands).
    • A — Attachment / Anchorage: Link intracellular cytoskeleton (microfilaments) to extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins (e.g., integrins, fibronectin, collagen) for structural stability.
    • C — Cell-Cell Recognition: Glycoproteins serve as molecular identification tags.
    • I — Intercellular Joining: Form tight junctions, desmosomes, and gap junctions between adjacent cells.
    • E — Enzymatic Activity: Catalyze sequential steps of a metabolic pathway right at the membrane surface.
Amino Acid Mutation FRQ Predictions: If a mutation replaces a nonpolar amino acid (e.g., Leucine/Valine) in the transmembrane domain of an integral protein with a charged/polar amino acid (e.g., Glutamate/Lysine), the protein cannot fold or embed properly into the hydrophobic fatty acid tail core!
Membrane Fluidity Control: Tails & Cholesterol Homeoviscous Adaptation
  • 1. Saturated vs. Unsaturated Fatty Acid Tails (All Cells):
    • Unsaturated Hydrocarbon Tails: Have one or more cis double bonds (C=C) that introduce kinks → prevent tight packing → INCREASE membrane fluidity (prevent freezing in cold environments).
    • Saturated Hydrocarbon Tails: Only single bonds (C–C); straight chains pack tightly together via van der Waals attractions → DECREASE fluidity (more viscous; adaptation for hot environments).
  • 2. Cholesterol (Temperature Fluidity Buffer in Animal Cell Membranes):
    • Steroid lipid wedged between phospholipid tails in animal plasma membranes (plants use phytosterols; prokaryotes lack cholesterol).
    • At High Temperatures (e.g., 37°C+): Restrains excessive phospholipid movement → REDUCES fluidity and stabilizes the membrane against melting/leakiness.
    • At Low Temperatures: Hinders close packing of fatty acid tails → PREVENTS solidification (freezing) and maintains fluidity.
Cholesterol = Temperature Fluidity Buffer!
  • High Temp: Restrains phospholipid movement → decreases fluidity (prevents melting).
  • Low Temp: Disrupts tight tail packing → prevents solidification (keeps membrane fluid).

Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 4 (Diffusion & Osmosis): Dialysis tubing & potato core mass change (% ΔMass = [(Final − Initial) / Initial] × 100) across sucrose molarities (0.0 M to 1.0 M). IV: Extracellular sucrose molarity (M); DV: % change in mass; Isotonic point: x-intercept where % ΔMass = 0% (no net water movement). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).

Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane. Impaired Cl⁻ efflux prevents osmotic water movement into mucus, producing thick, dehydrated mucus in lungs and pancreatic ducts. Ouabain/Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, collapsing the electrochemical gradient required for secondary active cotransport.
