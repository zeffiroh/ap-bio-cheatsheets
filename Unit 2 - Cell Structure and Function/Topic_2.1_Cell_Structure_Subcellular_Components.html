Universal Cell Features & Ribosomes Common Ancestry
  • Universal Features of All Cells (Prokaryotes & Eukaryotes): Plasma membrane, cytosol (semifluid jelly), chromosomes (DNA), and ribosomes → strong evolutionary evidence of common ancestry (LUCA).
  • Ribosomes (Non-Membrane-Bound):
    • Composed of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) + proteins (two subunits: large & small; 70S in prokaryotes/mitochondria/chloroplasts vs. 80S in eukaryotic cytosol).
    • Function: Translate mRNA codons into polypeptide chains (protein synthesis).
    • Free Ribosomes (suspended in cytosol): Synthesize proteins that function within the cytosol (e.g., glycolysis enzymes, cytoskeletal proteins).
    • Bound Ribosomes (attached to Rough ER / nuclear envelope): Synthesize proteins destined for secretion (export), insertion into membranes, or lysosomes.
Ribosomes = protein factories of the cell. Because they lack a lipid membrane, they exist in every living organism across all three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya).
If an AP question describes a cell secreting peptide hormones (e.g., insulin) or embedding receptor proteins into the plasma membrane → immediately think Rough ER-bound ribosomes → Golgi → secretory vesicles!
Endomembrane System: Nucleus, ER & Golgi Protein & Lipid Traffic
  • Endomembrane System: Coordinated network of membranes (physically continuous or connected via transport vesicles) that synthesize, modify, package, and transport proteins & lipids.
    • Includes: Nuclear envelope, Rough & Smooth ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vacuoles, transport vesicles, and plasma membrane. (Excludes mitochondria, chloroplasts, peroxisomes!)
  • Nucleus & Nucleolus: Double-membrane nuclear envelope perforated by nuclear pores; houses linear DNA + histones (chromatin). Nucleolus synthesizes rRNA and assembles ribosomal subunits.
ER Type Key Structure Main Functions
Rough ER (RER) Studded with bound ribosomes; flattened cisternae continuous with nuclear envelope Synthesizes, folds & glycosylates secretory/membrane/lysosomal proteins; packages them into transport vesicles; adds membrane phospholipids
Smooth ER (SER) Branched tubular network; NO ribosomes Lipid & steroid hormone synthesis, carbohydrate metabolism, detoxification of drugs/poisons (liver), Ca²⁺ storage (sarcoplasmic reticulum in muscle)
  • Golgi Complex (Apparatus): Stack of flattened, polarized membranous sacs (cisternae):
    • Cis face (receiving side): Near ER; receives transport vesicles from Rough ER.
    • Medial & Trans face (shipping side): Modifies carbohydrate tags on glycoproteins (e.g., adds mannose-6-phosphate tag for lysosomes), folds/processes molecules, sorts, and packages them into vesicles for secretion or delivery.
Rough ER → proteins (secretion/membrane) | Smooth ER → lipids + detoxification + Ca²⁺ | Golgi = post office / packaging & sorting center (cis receives → trans ships).
CED Scope Boundary: Specific functions of Smooth ER in specialized cells and specific Golgi enzymes for synthesizing particular phospholipids or packaging peroxisomal vs. secretory enzymes are beyond the AP Exam scope.

Lysosomes, Vacuoles & Cytoskeleton Digestion & Support
  • Lysosomes (Acidic Recycling Centers):
    • Membrane-bound sacs containing hydrolytic enzymes (acid hydrolases) that function optimally at acidic pH (~4.5–5.0) maintained by proton (H⁺) pumps.
    • Intracellular Digestion (Phagocytosis): Fuse with food vacuoles/phagosomes to digest macromolecules & invading pathogens.
    • Autophagy: Recycle damaged/worn-out organelles into reusable monomers.
    • Apoptosis (Programmed Cell Death): Controlled release of hydrolytic/caspase enzymes sculptures digits during embryonic development or eliminates infected/cancerous cells.
Macrophages (white blood cells) engulf bacteria via phagocytosis and fuse the phagosome with lysosomes to destroy the pathogen; tadpole tails are resorbed via lysosomal apoptosis.
Vacuole Type Cell Type & Key Features Primary Biological Functions
Central Vacuole Plant cells; single huge compartment (up to 80% of volume) bounded by tonoplast Stores water, ions, pigments, & metabolic waste; generates turgor pressure against cell wall for plant support & cell growth
Food Vacuoles Animal cells & protists; smaller, temporary, more numerous Formed by phagocytosis; fuse with lysosomes for nutrient digestion & storage
Contractile Vacuole Freshwater protists (e.g., Paramecium, Amoeba) Osmoregulation: Actively pumps excess water out of cell to prevent osmotic lysis in hypotonic freshwater
  • Cytoskeleton & Centrosomes: Dynamic network of protein fibers:
    • Microtubules (tubulin dimers, Hollow, 25 nm): Largest; maintain cell shape, form mitotic spindle fibers (grown from centrosomes/centrioles), tracks for motor proteins (kinesin/dynein), & core of cilia/flagella (9+2 arrangement).
    • Microfilaments (actin filaments, 7 nm): Thinnest; muscle contraction (with myosin), amoeboid pseudopodia, cytoplasmic streaming, and cleavage furrow in cytokinesis.
    • Intermediate Filaments (keratin/lamins, 8–12 nm): Permanent tension-bearing cables; anchor nucleus & organelles, form nuclear lamina.
Mitochondria & Chloroplasts (Energy Organelles) Double Membrane + DNA
  • Mitochondria (Site of Aerobic Cellular Respiration):
    • Found in nearly ALL eukaryotic cells (including plants, animals, fungi, protists!).
    • Outer Membrane: Smooth, porous (contains porin proteins).
    • Inner Membrane (Cristae): Highly folded infoldings called cristae that dramatically increase surface area to embed more Electron Transport Chain (ETC) proteins & ATP synthase complexes → maximizes ATP synthesis.
    • Compartments: Intermembrane space (narrow region where H⁺ accumulates to low pH) and Mitochondrial Matrix (contains Krebs/citric acid cycle enzymes, mitochondrial circular DNA, & 70S ribosomes).
  • Chloroplasts (Site of Photosynthesis in Plants & Algae):
    • Bounded by a smooth outer membrane and inner membrane separated by a narrow intermembrane space.
    • Thylakoids & Grana: Internal system of flattened membranous sacs (thylakoids) stacked into grana; thylakoid membranes house chlorophyll, photosystems I & II, and ETC for the light-dependent reactions (pumping H⁺ into the thylakoid lumen).
    • Stroma: Fluid outside thylakoids containing Rubisco & enzymes for the Calvin cycle (carbon fixation), circular chloroplast DNA, and 70S ribosomes.
Complementary Energy Organelles: Chloroplasts capture solar light energy to synthesize organic sugars (CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂); Mitochondria oxidize organic sugars to release energy as ATP (C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP). Plant cells have BOTH!
Putting It All Together (Coordinated System):
  • Ribosome → polypeptide synthesis | Rough ER → protein folding & transport
  • Smooth ER → lipid synthesis & detox | Golgi → modify, package, & ship
  • Mitochondria → aerobic ATP production | Chloroplast → photosynthesis
  • Lysosome → acidic digestion, autophagy, & apoptosis | Vacuole → storage & turgor

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 4 (Diffusion & Osmosis): Dialysis tubing & potato core mass change (% ΔMass = [(Final − Initial) / Initial] × 100) across sucrose molarities (0.0 M to 1.0 M). IV: Extracellular sucrose molarity (M); DV: % change in mass; Isotonic point: x-intercept where % ΔMass = 0% (no net water movement). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane. Impaired Cl⁻ efflux prevents osmotic water movement into mucus, producing thick, dehydrated mucus in lungs and pancreatic ducts. Ouabain/Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, collapsing the electrochemical gradient required for secondary active cotransport.
