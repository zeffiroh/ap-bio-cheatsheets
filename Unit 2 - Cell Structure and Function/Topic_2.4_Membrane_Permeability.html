Selective Permeability & Role of the Hydrophobic Core The Bouncer Rule
  • Selective Permeability: The plasma membrane regulates the entry and exit of substances, allowing some molecules to cross freely while restricting others based on polarity, charge, and molecular size.
  • Role of the Hydrophobic Interior Core:
    • Nonpolar fatty acid tails form a hydrophobic core inside the phospholipid bilayer.
    • Allows: Nonpolar, hydrophobic molecules to dissolve into the lipid core and diffuse rapidly across the membrane without protein assistance.
    • Repels / Blocks: Charged ions (surrounded by hydration shells) and large polar molecules cannot penetrate the nonpolar interior on their own → require specific transport proteins.
Plasma Membrane = Bouncer at the Club (Selective Entry): "Like dissolves like" — nonpolar passes through the nonpolar core freely; polar/charged need a VIP pass (membrane transport protein)!
FRQ Must-Write Phrase: Whenever explaining why an ion (Na⁺, K⁺, H⁺) or polar molecule (glucose) cannot cross the membrane directly, ALWAYS explicitly state: "Because the interior core of the phospholipid bilayer is hydrophobic/nonpolar, it repels polar and charged substances."
What Can Cross the Lipid Bilayer? (Permeability Hierarchy) High-Yield Comparison
Molecule Class Examples Bilayer Permeability Transport Mechanism
1. Small Nonpolar Molecules O₂, CO₂, N₂ HIGH (Pass Freely & Rapidly); dissolve in hydrophobic tails Simple Diffusion directly through phospholipid bilayer (no protein, no ATP)
2. Hydrophobic / Lipid-Soluble Steroid hormones (estrogen, testosterone), hydrocarbons, fatty acids HIGH (Pass Readily); nonpolar rings/chains Simple Diffusion across membrane to bind intracellular receptors
3. Small Uncharged Polar Molecules H₂O, NH₃, glycerol, ethanol, urea LOW / LIMITED (Pass Slowly) in small amounts through transient gaps Slow simple diffusion + rapid Facilitated Diffusion (e.g., H₂O via aquaporins)
4. Large Polar Uncharged Molecules Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), sucrose, amino acids, nucleotides IMPERMEABLE (Cannot Pass Directly); too large & polar Facilitated Diffusion (GLUT carrier proteins) or Active Cotransport
5. Charged Ions (Any Size!) Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺, HCO₃⁻ VIRTUALLY IMPERMEABLE; charge + hydration shell blocked by tails Ion Channels (facilitated diffusion) or ATPase Pumps (active transport)
Membrane Permeability Spectrum Visualized Cross-Bilayer Trajectories
Passive Transport: Simple vs. Facilitated Diffusion No ATP Required
  • Passive Transport: Spontaneous movement of molecules down their concentration gradient ([High] → [Low]); ΔG < 0, requires NO metabolic energy (no ATP):
    • 1. Simple Diffusion: Movement of small, nonpolar molecules (O₂, CO₂, N₂, steroid lipids) directly across the phospholipid bilayer without any transport protein. Rate is directly proportional to solute concentration gradient (linear kinetics).
    • 2. Facilitated Diffusion: Passive movement of polar or charged molecules down their gradient assisted by specific transmembrane proteins:
      • Channel Proteins: Provide a hydrophilic tunnel/pore through the membrane core (e.g., aquaporins for rapid water transport; gated ion channels for Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻).
      • Carrier Proteins (Permeases): Bind a specific solute (e.g., glucose or amino acids) and undergo a conformational shape change to translocate it across the membrane.
  • Why Selective Permeability Matters for Homeostasis:
    • Maintains distinct internal cytosolic concentrations vs. extracellular environment.
    • Controls ion & voltage gradients (membrane potential), selective nutrient uptake, toxic waste removal, and intracellular pH/osmoregulation.
Cell Walls: Structural Support & Osmotic Protection Plants, Fungi & Prokaryotes
  • Cell Wall Overview: Rigid extracellular layer located outside the plasma membrane in plants, fungi, bacteria, archaea, and many algae. (Absent in animal cells, which have an extracellular matrix [ECM] of collagen/proteoglycans instead!)
  • Core Functions of the Cell Wall:
    • Structural Support & Shape: Maintains mechanical rigidity and cell shape against gravity.
    • Prevents Osmotic Lysis (Bursting): When water enters by osmosis in a hypotonic environment, the rigid wall pushes back to create turgor pressure (+Ψp) rather than bursting.
    • Permeability Barrier Note: Cell walls are porous and freely permeable to water, O₂/CO₂, and small dissolved solutes — the plasma membrane underneath is what actually performs selective permeability!
Taxonomic Group Diagnostic Cell Wall Polymer Chemical Monomer / Structure
Plants & Green Algae Cellulose (+ pectin & lignin in wood) Unbranched β-1,4-glucose polysaccharide microfibrils; perforated by plasmodesmata
Fungi (Yeasts, Molds) Chitin Nitrogen-containing polysaccharide (N-acetylglucosamine); same polymer as arthropod exoskeletons!
Bacteria (Eubacteria) Peptidoglycan Modified sugar chains cross-linked by short polypeptides (targeted by penicillin & lysozyme)
Archaea Pseudopeptidoglycan / S-layer proteins / polysaccharides Lacks true peptidoglycan (insensitive to penicillin)
Polymer Identification on the AP Exam:
  • Cell wall with Nitrogen (N) in a eukaryotic decomposer → Chitin (Fungi)
  • Cell wall with only C, H, O (β-glucose) in a photoautotroph → Cellulose (Plants)
  • Cell wall with sugars + amino acids in a prokaryote → Peptidoglycan (Bacteria)
Common Trap: Do NOT say the cell wall is "selectively permeable" like the plasma membrane! The cell wall is a relatively coarse, porous meshwork (like a rigid basket); selective permeability is strictly a property of the phospholipid plasma membrane.

Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 4 (Diffusion & Osmosis): Dialysis tubing & potato core mass change (% ΔMass = [(Final − Initial) / Initial] × 100) across sucrose molarities (0.0 M to 1.0 M). IV: Extracellular sucrose molarity (M); DV: % change in mass; Isotonic point: x-intercept where % ΔMass = 0% (no net water movement). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane. Impaired Cl⁻ efflux prevents osmotic water movement into mucus, producing thick, dehydrated mucus in lungs and pancreatic ducts. Ouabain/Digoxin inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, collapsing the electrochemical gradient required for secondary active cotransport.
