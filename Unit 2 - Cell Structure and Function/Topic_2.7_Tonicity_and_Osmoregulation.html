Osmosis & Tonicity: Direction of Water Movement Passive H₂O Flow
  • Osmosis: Passive diffusion of free water (H₂O) across a selectively permeable membrane (mainly through aquaporins); requires NO ATP.
  • Universal Rule for Direction of Net Water Flow (3 Equivalent Ways to State It):
    • 1. Water Potential (Ψ): From HIGH water potential (Ψ) → LOW (more negative) water potential (Ψ).
    • 2. Solute Concentration: From LOW osmolarity (solute concentration) → HIGH osmolarity (solute concentration) ("Solute sucks!").
    • 3. Tonicity: From a HYPOTONIC solution → HYPERTONIC solution across a membrane.
FRQ Precision Tip: Always state that water moves by osmosis "from a region of higher water potential (Ψ) / lower solute concentration to a region of lower water potential (Ψ) / higher solute concentration" — don't just say "toward solute"!
External Solution Tonicity Net H₂O Direction Animal Cell (No Cell Wall) Plant Cell (Rigid Cell Wall)
Hypotonic Solution
(Lower solute outside; higher Ψ outside)		 Net H₂O ENTERS cell Lysed (Bursts / Cytolysis); swells until plasma membrane ruptures Turgid (Normal / Ideal!); central vacuole swells & pushes plasma membrane against cell wall (+Ψp)
Isotonic Solution
(Equal solute & equal Ψ inside/outside)		 No net H₂O movement (equal rates in & out) Normal (Ideal!); e.g., RBCs in 0.9% NaCl saline Flaccid (Limp); no turgor pressure (Ψp = 0), plant wilts
Hypertonic Solution
(Higher solute outside; lower/more neg Ψ outside)		 Net H₂O LEAVES cell Shriveled (Crenated); dehydrates & shrinks Plasmolyzed (Wilted/Lethal); plasma membrane pulls away from cell wall as vacuole shrinks

Water Potential (Ψ) & Solute Potential Equations AP Math Must-Know
  • Water Potential (Ψ, measured in bars or MPa): Quantifies the potential energy of water to move from one area to another due to osmosis, gravity, and mechanical pressure.
Ψ = Ψp + Ψs    |    Ψs = −iCRT
  • Pressure Potential (Ψp): Physical/hydrostatic pressure on solution:
    • In an Open Beaker / Animal Cell: Ψp = 0 bars (at atmospheric pressure).
    • In a Turgid Plant Cell: Ψp > 0 (positive turgor pressure exerted by cell wall pushing back).
    • In Plant Xylem Vessels (Transpirational Pull): Ψp < 0 (negative tension/suction).
  • Solute Potential (Ψs, Osmotic Potential): Effect of dissolved solutes on water potential:
    • Pure Water in Open Beaker: Ψs = 0 and Ψp = 0 → Ψ = 0 bars (MAXIMUM possible water potential at 1 atm!).
    • Adding Solute ALWAYS Lowers Ψs: Solute binds water molecules via hydration shells, reducing free water → Ψs is ALWAYS 0 or NEGATIVE (≤ 0). More solute = more negative Ψs!
  • Variables in Ψs = −iCRT:
    • i = Ionization constant (number of particles formed per formula unit in water):
      • Covalent non-electrolytes (Glucose, Sucrose): Do NOT ionize → i = 1.0.
      • Ionic salts: NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ (i = 2); CaCl₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ (i = 3).
    • C = Molar concentration (mol/L or M).
    • R = Pressure constant = 0.0831 liter·bar / (mol·K).
    • T = Absolute temperature in Kelvin (K = °C + 273).
Worked AP Exam Calculation Example:
Find the water potential (Ψ) of an open beaker containing 0.3 M sucrose at 27°C:
1. Convert Temp: T = 27 + 273 = 300 K; for sucrose, i = 1.0; open beaker Ψp = 0.
2. Calculate Ψs = −(1.0)(0.3 mol/L)(0.0831 L·bar/mol·K)(300 K) = −7.48 bars.
3. Total Ψ = Ψp + Ψs = 0 + (−7.48) = −7.48 bars. If a plant cell at equilibrium in this beaker has Ψs = −9.0 bars, its turgor pressure is Ψp = Ψ − Ψs = −7.48 − (−9.0) = +1.52 bars!
Golden Rules of Water Potential (Ψ):
  • Pure water in an open container has Ψ = 0.
  • More dissolved solute → more negative Ψs → lower total Ψ.
  • Water ALWAYS moves passively toward the more negative Ψ (e.g., from Ψ = −1.5 bars to Ψ = −4.0 bars)!
Osmoregulation: Maintaining Water & Solute Balance Organism Adaptations
  • Osmoregulation: Active regulation of internal water volume and solute concentrations (osmolarity) to maintain homeostasis, cell volume survival, and optimal enzyme conformation.
Organism / Habitat Osmotic Challenge Key Osmoregulatory Adaptations
Freshwater Protists & Fish
(Hypotonic environment)		 Water constantly enters cells by osmosis; salts lost by diffusion Contractile vacuole (in Paramecium/Amoeba) uses ATP to pump water out; freshwater fish do not drink, excrete large volumes of very dilute urine, & gills actively uptake ions
Marine (Saltwater) Bony Fish
(Hypertonic environment)		 Water constantly leaves body via gills; salt diffuses in Constantly drink seawater, actively pump excess Na⁺/Cl⁻ out across gill chloride cells, & excrete tiny amounts of concentrated urine
Terrestrial Animals Dehydration via evaporation & excretion Waterproof skin/cuticle; mammalian kidneys (Loop of Henle + ADH) reabsorb water to produce hyperosmotic urine
Vascular Plants Transpirational water loss vs. turgor support Central vacuole accumulates solutes (lowers Ψs) to draw water in; guard cells close stomata during drought (via hormone ABA)

II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Graph Rules: Total Water Potential: Ψ = Ψ_p + Ψ_s. Solute Potential: Ψ_s = −iCRT (where i = ionization constant [1 for sucrose/glucose, 2 for NaCl], C = molarity in mol/L, R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K, T = K = °C + 273). Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio (SA:V): Cube SA = 6s², V = s³ (SA:V = 6/s). Larger SA:V accelerates diffusion per unit volume. Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).

Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Physiological Disruption Case Study: Cystic Fibrosis results from a deletion (ΔF508) in the CFTR chloride channel protein, preventing misfolded channels from reaching the apical plasma membrane.
