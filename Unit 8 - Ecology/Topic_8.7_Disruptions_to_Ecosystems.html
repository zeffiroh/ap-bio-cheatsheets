1. Random Mutations, Adaptations & Environmental Shifts CED EVO-1.O
  • An Adaptation Is a Genetic Variation Favored by Selection: Increases survival or reproductive fitness in a particular environment.
  • Mutations Are Random (Not Directed by Need!):
    • The environment does NOT create beneficial mutations on demand.
    • Random mutations generate genetic variation prior to environmental change; when an ecosystem is disrupted, natural selection acts on pre-existing variation!
  • Heterozygote Advantage Maintains Alleles: When the heterozygous genotype has higher fitness than either homozygote (e.g., Sickle-cell trait HbA/HbS providing malaria resistance in endemic regions), harmful recessive alleles remain preserved in the gene pool.
Never say organisms mutate "because they needed to adapt" to pollution or climate change! Individuals with pre-existing tolerance alleles survive the disruption and reproduce; populations lacking those alleles decline or go extinct.
2. Invasive Species: Mechanisms & Ecological Impacts Unchecked Exponential Growth
  • Invasive Species: Non-native (exotic/introduced) species intentionally or accidentally transported into a new ecosystem where they spread rapidly and disrupt native communities.
  • Why Invasive Species Succeed So Dramatically (3 Key Reasons):
    • 1. Lack of Natural Predators, Parasites, or Pathogens: Escaped top-down population control from their native range ("enemy release").
    • 2. Exploit Unused Niches or Outcompete Natives: Superior resource extraction + naive native prey that lack co-evolved anti-predator defenses.
    • 3. High Reproductive Rate (r-Selected Traits): Rapid maturation, huge brood sizes, and generalist diet/habitat tolerance → initial exponential (J-curve) growth!
High-Yield AP Invasive Species Case Studies:
  • Kudzu Vine (SE US): Introduced from Japan to prevent soil erosion → grows up to 1 ft/day, smothering native forest trees and blocking sunlight.
  • Zebra Mussels (Great Lakes): Arrived in ship ballast water → filter out phytoplankton, starving native mussels and clogging water intake pipes.
  • Cane Toads (Australia): Introduced to eat cane beetles → secrete fatal cardiotoxic bufotoxin that kills native marsupial quolls, monitor lizards, and snakes that try to eat them.
  • Brown Tree Snake (Guam) & Burmese Python (Everglades): Decimated native island forest birds and Everglades marsh mammals.
3. Geological, Meteorological & Climate Disruptions Abiotic & Global Change
  • Geological & Meteorological Events Alter Habitats & Distributions:
    • El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO): Warm Pacific surface water suppresses cold, nutrient-rich coastal upwelling along South America → phytoplankton crash → anchovy fish & seabird/marine mammal populations plummet.
    • Continental Drift & Meteor Impacts: Shift climate zones, isolate clades, or trigger mass extinctions.
  • Anthropogenic Climate Change, Ocean Acidification & Habitat Loss:
    • Habitat Fragmentation: Roads, logging, and agriculture split large habitats into small, isolated patches — reducing population size (N↓), blocking gene flow, increasing genetic drift/inbreeding, and creating harmful edge effects (solved by wildlife corridors!).
    • Ocean Acidification: Excess atmospheric CO₂ dissolves in seawater to form carbonic acid (CO₂ + H₂O ↔ H₂CO₃ ↔ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻), lowering ocean pH and depleting carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻) needed by corals and mollusks to build CaCO₃ shells/skeletons; warm water also triggers coral bleaching (expulsion of mutualistic zooxanthellae algae).
4. Human Disruptions: Eutrophication & Biomagnification High-Yield Step Pathways
  • Cultural Eutrophication: Agricultural fertilizer (N, P) and sewage runoff trigger explosive algal blooms; when algae die, aerobic decomposer bacteria consume dissolved oxygen (O₂) via cellular respiration, creating hypoxic "dead zones" (e.g., Gulf of Mexico) that suffocate fish and shellfish.
  • Bioaccumulation vs. Biomagnification:
    • Bioaccumulation: Gradual buildup of a persistent toxin within a single organism's tissues over its lifetime.
    • Biomagnification: Increasing concentration of persistent, fat-soluble toxins (DDT, PCBs, methylmercury) in tissues at successively higher trophic levels in a food chain (e.g., DDT caused fragile eggshell thinning in bald eagles and peregrine falcons, documented in Rachel Carson's Silent Spring).

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 10, 11 & 12 (Energy Dynamics, Whole-Plant Transpiration & Pillbug Animal Behavior Choice Chambers): Choice chambers testing pillbug (Armadillidium vulgare) chemotaxis/hydrotaxis/kinesis (count pillbugs on wet vs. dry filter paper every 30 s for 10 min; evaluate using χ² against 50:50 null distribution). Potometer/gravimetric transpiration across light, wind, and humidity treatments normalized per cm² leaf surface area. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Ecological Equations: Population Growth Rate: dN/dt = B − D. Exponential Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N (J-curve). Logistic Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N·[(K − N)/K] (S-curve; maximum instantaneous growth rate dN/dt occurs at N = K/2). Simpson's Diversity Index: D_I = 1 − Σ(n / N)² (ranges from 0 to 1; higher value = higher diversity). Net Primary Productivity: NPP = GPP − R. Trophic 10% Rule: ~10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level (~90% lost as metabolic heat/respiration). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Ecological Disruption & Trophic Cascade Case Study: Removal of a keystone predator (e.g., Pisaster ochraceus sea star in rocky intertidal zones, or sea otters in kelp forests) triggers a top-down trophic cascade: prey populations (mussels or sea urchins) explode unchecked, competitively excluding other invertebrates or overgrazing kelp producers, collapsing Simpson's Diversity Index. Similarly, agricultural N/P fertilizer runoff causes Cultural Eutrophication: algal blooms block light, aerobic bacteria decompose dead algae and deplete dissolved O₂, creating hypoxic dead zones.
