1. Endotherms vs. Ectotherms & Metabolic Scaling Organismal Bioenergetics
Feature Endotherms (Mammals, Birds) Ectotherms (Reptiles, Amphibians, Fish, Inverts)
Heat Source Generate internal thermal energy via high cellular respiration / metabolic rate (plus mitochondrial uncoupling proteins in brown fat). Rely primarily on external environmental heat; regulate body temperature behaviorally (basking vs. shade).
Body Temp & Energy Maintain constant internal temperature across wide ambient temps, but require much higher food/caloric intake. Body temperature & metabolic rate rise/fall with ambient temp; require far less food energy (can survive long fasts).
  • Body Size vs. Mass-Specific Metabolic Rate (Surface-Area-to-Volume Ratio):
    • Smaller endotherms (e.g., mouse, shrew, hummingbird) have a high SA:V ratio, losing body heat rapidly to the environment → require a much higher metabolic rate per gram of body tissue (higher breathing, heart rate, and O₂ consumption/g) than a large elephant!
  • Net Energy Gain vs. Net Energy Loss & Reproductive Strategy:
    • Net Energy Gain (Intake > Expenditure): Excess free energy goes to energy storage, growth, and higher reproductive output.
    • Net Energy Loss (Expenditure > Intake): Loss of biomass, halted reproduction, and eventual death.
    • Asexual vs. Sexual Switching: Organisms like Daphnia (water fleas) and aphids reproduce asexually (fast, low energy cost) when food/conditions are abundant and stable, and switch to sexual reproduction (higher energy cost, generates genetic variation & hardy resting eggs) under environmental stress/crowding!
Ectotherms are NOT "inactive" or "inferior"! At warm temperatures they are highly active and convert a much higher fraction of ingested food into new biomass because they don't burn 80–90% of their calories just to stay warm.
2. Autotrophs, Heterotrophs & Primary Productivity GPP vs. NPP
  • Autotrophs (Primary Producers): Form the base of every ecosystem by converting inorganic carbon (CO₂) into organic biomass:
    • Photoautotrophs: Plants, algae, and cyanobacteria capture solar light energy via photosynthesis.
    • Chemoautotrophs: Deep-sea hydrothermal vent prokaryote bacteria oxidize inorganic molecules (H₂S, NH₃, Fe²⁺) in total darkness without sunlight!
  • Heterotrophs (Consumers & Decomposers): Obtain free energy by consuming organic compounds produced by other organisms (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, scavengers, and decomposers [fungi & bacteria]).
  • Gross vs. Net Primary Productivity (GPP vs. NPP):
NPP = GPP − R   (Net Primary Productivity = Gross Primary Productivity − Autotroph Respiration)
  • GPP: Total solar energy captured and converted into chemical energy (glucose) by photosynthesis per unit area per time.
  • NPP: The remaining energy stored as new plant biomass after producers use R for their own cellular respiration — NPP is the ONLY energy available to primary consumers!
3. Trophic Levels & The 10% Rule of Ecological Efficiency Energy Flows, Matter Cycles
  • 10% Rule of Ecological Efficiency: On average, only ~10% of the chemical energy stored in one trophic level is converted into biomass at the next higher trophic level. The remaining ~90% is lost as metabolic heat (Second Law of Thermodynamics), cellular respiration, movement, and undigested feces.
  • Why Food Chains Are Short (4–5 Trophic Levels Max): So little energy remains by the 4th/5th level that top-predator populations are small and highly vulnerable to producer decline.
ENERGY FLOWS ONE-WAY; MATTER CYCLES! Energy enters ecosystems as sunlight and exits as heat (never recycled!). Chemical elements (C, H, O, N, P) are conserved and recycled endlessly between biotic and abiotic reservoirs by decomposers.
4. Major Biogeochemical Cycles (Water, C, N, P) Matter Recycling
Cycle Main Abiotic Reservoir Key Biological & Physical Processes
Hydrologic (H₂O) Oceans (~97%), glaciers, groundwater, atmosphere Driven by solar energy: Evaporation, Plant Transpiration, Condensation, Precipitation, Runoff/Percolation.
Carbon (C) Atmosphere (CO₂), oceans (HCO₃⁻), fossil fuels, limestone Photosynthesis fixes CO₂ into organic glucose; Cellular Respiration, Decomposition, & Fossil Fuel Combustion return CO₂ to air.
Nitrogen (N) Atmosphere (N₂ gas = 78%; triple bond N≡N unusable by plants!) Prokaryotes required at every step: (1) N₂ Fixation (Rhizobium / cyanobacteria: N₂ → NH₃/NH₄⁺); (2) Nitrification (NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻); (3) Assimilation by plants; (4) Ammonification (decomposers); (5) Denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂).
Phosphorus (P) Sedimentary Rock & Soil/Water (NO atmospheric gas phase!) Weathering of rock releases phosphate (PO₄³⁻) into soil/water → absorbed by plants (for DNA, RNA, ATP, phospholipids) → consumers → decomposers → sedimentation.

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 10, 11 & 12 (Energy Dynamics, Whole-Plant Transpiration & Pillbug Animal Behavior Choice Chambers): Choice chambers testing pillbug (Armadillidium vulgare) chemotaxis/hydrotaxis/kinesis (count pillbugs on wet vs. dry filter paper every 30 s for 10 min; evaluate using χ² against 50:50 null distribution). Potometer/gravimetric transpiration across light, wind, and humidity treatments normalized per cm² leaf surface area. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Ecological Equations: Population Growth Rate: dN/dt = B − D. Exponential Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N (J-curve). Logistic Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N·[(K − N)/K] (S-curve; maximum instantaneous growth rate dN/dt occurs at N = K/2). Simpson's Diversity Index: D_I = 1 − Σ(n / N)² (ranges from 0 to 1; higher value = higher diversity). Net Primary Productivity: NPP = GPP − R. Trophic 10% Rule: ~10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level (~90% lost as metabolic heat/respiration). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Ecological Disruption & Trophic Cascade Case Study: Removal of a keystone predator (e.g., Pisaster ochraceus sea star in rocky intertidal zones, or sea otters in kelp forests) triggers a top-down trophic cascade: prey populations (mussels or sea urchins) explode unchecked, competitively excluding other invertebrates or overgrazing kelp producers, collapsing Simpson's Diversity Index. Similarly, agricultural N/P fertilizer runoff causes Cultural Eutrophication: algal blooms block light, aerobic bacteria decompose dead algae and deplete dissolved O₂, creating hypoxic dead zones.
