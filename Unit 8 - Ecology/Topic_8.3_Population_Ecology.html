1. Population Structure, Dispersion & Birth/Death Equation Demographics
  • Population: A group of individuals of the same species living and interbreeding in the same geographic area at the same time.
  • 3 Spatial Dispersion Patterns:
    • 1. Clumped (Most Common): Individuals aggregate in patches around localized resources (water holes, nutrients), social herding/pack defense, or clumped offspring dispersal.
    • 2. Uniform: Evenly spaced due to territoriality (nesting penguins) or chemical inhibition (allelopathy in desert sagebrush).
    • 3. Random: Unpredictable spacing in homogeneous environments without strong attractions/repulsions (e.g., wind-blown dandelion seeds).
  • Fundamental Population Growth Equation (AP Formula Sheet):
dN / dt = B − D   (Change in Population Size over Time = Births − Deaths)
  • If B > D → population grows (dN/dt > 0); if B < D → population declines (dN/dt < 0); if B = D → zero population growth (dN/dt = 0). (In open populations, Immigration + I and Emigration − E also contribute).
2. Exponential (J-Curve) vs. Logistic (S-Curve) Growth AP Formula Sheet Math
Exponential: dN/dt = r_max · N   |   Logistic: dN/dt = r_max · N · [ (K − N) / K ]
  • Variables Defined:
    • N = Current population size  |  t = Time  |  dN/dt = Population growth rate (individuals added per unit time)
    • r_max = Maximum per capita growth rate of population (intrinsic rate of increase under ideal conditions: r = (B − D)/N)
    • K = Carrying Capacity (maximum population size the environment can sustainably support)
  • Exponential Growth (J-Shaped Curve):
    • Occurs when resources are unlimited / abundant and constraints (predators/disease) are absent (e.g., bacteria in fresh broth, invasive species entering a new habitat, or population rebounding after a bottleneck).
    • Notice: Per capita growth rate (r_max) stays constant, while overall population growth rate (dN/dt) accelerates continuously as N gets larger!
  • Logistic Growth (S-Shaped / Sigmoidal Curve):
    • Occurs when limited resources slow growth as N approaches carrying capacity K.
    • The term (K − N) / K represents the fraction of K still available for growth:
      • When N is very small (N ≪ K): (K−N)/K ≈ 1 → growth is nearly exponential.
      • When N = K / 2 (Inflection Point): Population growth rate dN/dt is at its ABSOLUTE MAXIMUM!
      • When N = K: (K−N)/K = 0 → dN/dt = 0 (population stabilizes at carrying capacity).
      • When N > K (Overshoot): (K−N)/K < 0 → dN/dt becomes negative, and the population declines back toward K.

Worked AP Logistic Growth Calculation:
A rabbit population has r_max = 0.5 per month, carrying capacity K = 400, and current population size N = 300. Calculate dN/dt and the predicted population size after 1 month:
  • dN/dt = 0.5 × 300 × [(400 − 300) / 400] = 150 × (100 / 400) = 150 × 0.25 = +37.5 ≈ +38 rabbits/month.
  • New Population Size (N_new): 300 + 38 = 338 rabbits! (Always check if the question asks for growth rate dN/dt or total new population size N + dN!)
3. Life History Strategies: r-Selected vs. K-Selected Survivorship Trade-Offs
Life History Trait r-Selected (Opportunistic) K-Selected (Equilibrium)
Typical Environment Unstable, unpredictable, or disturbed habitats (low competition initially) Stable, predictable habitats near carrying capacity K (intense competition)
Offspring Number & Size Many small offspring per reproductive event; few survive Few large offspring; high probability of surviving to adulthood
Maturation & Care Early maturity, short lifespan, little or no parental care (often semelparous — 1 big brood) Late maturity, long lifespan, extensive parental investment (iteroparous — repeated reproduction)
Survivorship Curve Type III Curve: High juvenile mortality; survivors live long (insects, weeds, oysters, frogs) Type I Curve: High survival in youth/middle age; mortality in old age (humans, elephants, whales)

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigations 10, 11 & 12 (Energy Dynamics, Whole-Plant Transpiration & Pillbug Animal Behavior Choice Chambers): Choice chambers testing pillbug (Armadillidium vulgare) chemotaxis/hydrotaxis/kinesis (count pillbugs on wet vs. dry filter paper every 30 s for 10 min; evaluate using χ² against 50:50 null distribution). Potometer/gravimetric transpiration across light, wind, and humidity treatments normalized per cm² leaf surface area. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Ecological Equations: Population Growth Rate: dN/dt = B − D. Exponential Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N (J-curve). Logistic Growth: dN/dt = r_max·N·[(K − N)/K] (S-curve; maximum instantaneous growth rate dN/dt occurs at N = K/2). Simpson's Diversity Index: D_I = 1 − Σ(n / N)² (ranges from 0 to 1; higher value = higher diversity). Net Primary Productivity: NPP = GPP − R. Trophic 10% Rule: ~10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level (~90% lost as metabolic heat/respiration). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Ecological Disruption & Trophic Cascade Case Study: Removal of a keystone predator (e.g., Pisaster ochraceus sea star in rocky intertidal zones, or sea otters in kelp forests) triggers a top-down trophic cascade: prey populations (mussels or sea urchins) explode unchecked, competitively excluding other invertebrates or overgrazing kelp producers, collapsing Simpson's Diversity Index. Similarly, agricultural N/P fertilizer runoff causes Cultural Eutrophication: algal blooms block light, aerobic bacteria decompose dead algae and deplete dissolved O₂, creating hypoxic dead zones.
