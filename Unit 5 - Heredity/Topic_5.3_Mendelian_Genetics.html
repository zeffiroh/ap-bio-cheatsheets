Mendel’s Two Laws & Meiotic Chromosome Basis Chromosomal Basis
  • 1. Law of Segregation:
    • The two alleles for a heritable character separate (segregate) during gamete formation so that each gamete receives only 1 allele for each gene.
    • Meiotic Basis: Physical separation of homologous chromosomes during Anaphase I (and sister chromatids in Anaphase II).
  • 2. Law of Independent Assortment:
    • Alleles of two (or more) different genes assort independently into gametes; inheriting one allele does not affect inheritance of another gene.
    • Meiotic Basis: Random alignment of homologous chromosome pairs along the metaphase plate during Metaphase I.
  • Fertilization Restores Ploidy: Meiosis halves chromosome number (2n → n); fertilization fuses two haploid gametes (n + n → 2n) to restore the diploid state and create novel allele combinations in the zygote.
When Independent Assortment Applies: Genes assort independently when located on different chromosomes (unlinked) or very far apart (RF = 50%) on the same chromosome.
Genetic Vocabulary & Standard Mendelian Crosses 3:1, 9:3:3:1, Testcross
  • Genotype vs. Phenotype: Genotype = actual allele combination (TT, Tt, tt); Phenotype = observable physical/biochemical trait (TT and Tt share the same dominant phenotype; only tt shows the recessive phenotype).
  • Homozygous vs. Heterozygous: Homozygous = identical alleles (AA dominant or aa recessive); Heterozygous = two different alleles (Aa).

Dominant ≠ Most Common in a Population: Dominant only means the allele masks the recessive allele in a heterozygote (Aa). A dominant allele (like Huntington's disease or polydactyly) can be rare in a population!
Rules of Probability in Multi-Gene Crosses Skip Giant Punnett Squares!
  • Multiplication Rule (“AND” Rule — Independent Events):
    • Probability that two or more independent events occur together equals the product of their individual probabilities: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).
  • Addition Rule (“OR” Rule — Mutually Exclusive Events):
    • Probability that an event can occur in two or more mutually exclusive ways equals the sum of their individual probabilities: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
Fast Trihybrid Cross (AaBbCc × AaBbcc): What is the probability of an aabbcc offspring? Treat each gene as a separate monohybrid cross and multiply: P(aa) = ¼, P(bb) = ¼, P(cc) = ½ → ¼ × ¼ × ½ = 1/32!
Pedigree Analysis: Rapid Diagnostic Flowchart Autosomal vs. Sex-Linked
  • Symbols: Squares = Males; Circles = Females; Shaded = Affected; Unshaded = Unaffected.
  • Step 1 — Does the trait skip generations?
    • Two unaffected parents have an affected child → Trait MUST be Recessive (parents are heterozygous carriers, Aa × Aa → aa).
    • Two affected parents have an unaffected child → Trait MUST be Dominant (parents are heterozygotes, Aa × Aa → aa).
  • Step 2 — Check Sex Bias & Father-to-Son Transmission:
    • Autosomal Recessive: Affects males & females equally; skips generations (e.g., Cystic fibrosis, Tay-Sachs, PKU).
    • Autosomal Dominant: Affects males & females equally; appears in every generation; affected child always has ≥1 affected parent (e.g., Huntington's, Achondroplasia).
    • X-Linked Recessive: Mainly affects males (XᵃY); NO father-to-son transmission (fathers give Y to sons); affected female (XᵃXᵃ) must have an affected father and a carrier/affected mother.
    • X-Linked Dominant: Affected father (XᴬY) passes trait to 100% of his daughters and 0% of his sons.
Chi-Square (χ²) Goodness-of-Fit Test in Genetics AP Formula Sheet
χ² = Σ [ (Observed − Expected)² / Expected ]   |   Degrees of Freedom: df = n − 1
  • Purpose: Tests whether deviations between Observed (O) offspring counts and Expected (E) Mendelian counts are due to random chance or a different inheritance pattern (e.g., linkage).
  • Null Hypothesis (H₀): There is no statistically significant difference between observed and expected counts (the data follow the predicted Mendelian ratio; any difference is due to chance).
  • Degrees of Freedom (df): Number of phenotypic categories minus 1 (df = n − 1). Use critical value at p = 0.05:
    • If χ² < Critical Value → Fail to reject H₀ (data fit Mendelian model; deviations are random chance).
    • If χ² ≥ Critical Value → Reject H₀ (data do NOT follow Mendelian model; e.g., genes are linked!).
Worked χ² Example: In a dihybrid test cross (AaBb × aabb, N = 400 total offspring), expected 1:1:1:1 ratio gives E = 100 for each of the 4 classes (df = 4 − 1 = 3, critical value at p=0.05 is 7.82). If observed counts are 170, 170, 30, 30, then χ² = 49 + 49 + 49 + 49 = 196 > 7.82 → Reject H₀ (genes are linked on the same chromosome!).

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 Part 2 (Sordaria fimicola Meiotic Crossing Over) & Drosophila/Fast Plants Crosses: Counting 4:4 non-recombinant vs. 2:2:2:2 / 2:4:2 recombinant asci in Sordaria perithecia to determine gene-to-centromere map distance (% Crossover = [Recombinant asci / Total asci] × 100; Map distance in cM = % Crossover / 2 because only 2 of 4 chromatids in a tetrad cross over). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Statistical Testing: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit: χ² = Σ [(O − E)² / E], with Degrees of Freedom df = (number of phenotypic classes − 1). At α = 0.05: if χ² > critical value, REJECT H₀ (e.g., genes are autosomal linked rather than assorting independently 9:3:3:1); if χ² ≤ critical value, FAIL TO REJECT H₀. Recombination Frequency: RF = (Total Recombinants / Total Offspring) × 100% = Map Units (cM). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Chromosomal Disruption Case Study: Meiotic Nondisjunction (failure of homologous chromosomes to separate in Anaphase I, or sister chromatids to separate in Anaphase II) yields aneuploid gametes (n + 1 or n − 1). Fertilization produces Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome, 47, +21), Klinefelter Syndrome (47, XXY), or monosomic Turner Syndrome (45, X0). Mitochondrial myopathies (e.g., Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy) exhibit strict maternal inheritance because the ovum contributes virtually all zygotic cytoplasm and mitochondria.
