Purpose of Meiosis & Chromosome Terminology 2n → n Reduction
  • Core Function: Produces haploid (n) gametes from diploid (2n) germ cells so chromosome number is halved before fertilization (preventing chromosome doubling every generation).
  • Biological Importance: Essential for sexual reproduction, chromosome number stability across generations, and generating genetic variation (via crossing over, independent assortment, and random fertilization).
  • Homologous Chromosomes vs. Sister Chromatids:
    • Homologous Pair (Homologs): Two chromosomes of the same length, centromere position, and gene loci — 1 maternal + 1 paternal (may carry different alleles).
    • Sister Chromatids: Two identical copies of a single chromosome synthesized during Interphase S-phase and held together at the centromere by cohesin proteins.
Meiosis = Chromosome Reduction + Genetic Shuffling: Preceded by 1 round of DNA replication (S phase) followed by 2 consecutive nuclear divisions (Meiosis I and Meiosis II).
Meiosis I: Reductional Division (Homologs Separate) 2n (4C) → 1n (2C)
  • Prophase I (Longest & Most Complex Stage):
    • Chromatin condenses; nuclear envelope breaks down; meiotic spindle forms.
    • Synapsis: Homologous chromosomes pair up gene-for-gene via the synaptonemal complex to form a tetrad (bivalent = 4 chromatids).
    • Crossing Over: Non-sister chromatids break and swap corresponding DNA segments at X-shaped chiasmata, creating recombinant chromatids.
  • Metaphase I: Homologous pairs align side-by-side (in pairs / two rows) along the metaphase plate. Maternal and paternal homologs orient randomly toward either pole (drives independent assortment).
  • Anaphase I: Homologous chromosomes separate and move to opposite poles pulled by kinetochore microtubules; sister chromatids remain attached at their centromeres.
  • Telophase I & Cytokinesis: Chromosomes reach poles, nuclear envelopes may reform, and cytoplasm divides → yields 2 haploid (n) daughter cells, but each chromosome still consists of 2 duplicated sister chromatids.
  • Prophase I = Pairing (synapsis) + Swapping (crossing over at chiasmata)
  • Metaphase I = Homologous pairs line up as pairs (tetrads at plate)
  • After Meiosis I = Cells are already haploid (n) because homolog pairs are separated, even though chromosomes are still duplicated (2C)!
No Interphase S-Phase Between Divisions: There is NO DNA replication between Meiosis I and Meiosis II (interkinesis lacks S phase).

Meiosis II: Equational Division (Sisters Separate) 1n (2C) → 1n (1C)
  • Mechanistically resembles mitosis in a haploid cell — separates sister chromatids:
  • Prophase II: Spindle apparatus reforms in both haploid cells; nuclear envelopes break down; chromosomes attach to microtubules (NO synapsis or crossing over).
  • Metaphase II: Chromosomes align individually (single file) along the metaphase plate; sister kinetochores attach to spindle fibers from opposite poles.
  • Anaphase II: Centromeric cohesin is cleaved → sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite poles as individual unduplicated chromosomes.
  • Telophase II & Cytokinesis: Nuclear envelopes reform, chromosomes decondense into chromatin, and cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm.
  • Final Outcome: Produces 4 genetically unique haploid (n) cells, each containing unduplicated chromosomes.
    • Human Spermatogenesis (Males): Produces 4 functional haploid sperm.
    • Human Oogenesis (Females): Asymmetric cytokinesis produces 1 large functional ovum (egg) (retaining cytoplasm/organelles) + 3 small polar bodies that degenerate.
Anaphase I vs. Anaphase II: In Anaphase I, homologous chromosomes separate (centromeres stay intact). In Anaphase II, centromeres split and sister chromatids separate!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 Part 2 (Sordaria fimicola Meiotic Crossing Over) & Drosophila/Fast Plants Crosses: Counting 4:4 non-recombinant vs. 2:2:2:2 / 2:4:2 recombinant asci in Sordaria perithecia to determine gene-to-centromere map distance (% Crossover = [Recombinant asci / Total asci] × 100; Map distance in cM = % Crossover / 2 because only 2 of 4 chromatids in a tetrad cross over). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Statistical Testing: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit: χ² = Σ [(O − E)² / E], with Degrees of Freedom df = (number of phenotypic classes − 1). At α = 0.05: if χ² > critical value, REJECT H₀ (e.g., genes are autosomal linked rather than assorting independently 9:3:3:1); if χ² ≤ critical value, FAIL TO REJECT H₀. Recombination Frequency: RF = (Total Recombinants / Total Offspring) × 100% = Map Units (cM). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Chromosomal Disruption Case Study: Meiotic Nondisjunction (failure of homologous chromosomes to separate in Anaphase I, or sister chromatids to separate in Anaphase II) yields aneuploid gametes (n + 1 or n − 1). Fertilization produces Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome, 47, +21), Klinefelter Syndrome (47, XXY), or monosomic Turner Syndrome (45, X0). Mitochondrial myopathies (e.g., Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy) exhibit strict maternal inheritance because the ovum contributes virtually all zygotic cytoplasm and mitochondria.
