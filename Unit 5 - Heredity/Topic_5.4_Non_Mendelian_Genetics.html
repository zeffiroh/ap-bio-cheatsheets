Genetic Linkage & Chromosome Mapping (cM) Violates 9:3:3:1 / 1:1:1:1
  • Linked Genes: Genes located physically close together on the same chromosome do NOT assort independently and tend to be inherited together during meiosis.
  • How Crossing Over Breaks Linkage: During Prophase I, homologous recombination exchanges segments between non-sister chromatids, producing a minority of recombinant gametes alongside the majority of parental gametes.
  • Distance ↔ Recombination Rule:
    • Closer genes → lower probability of a crossover between them (“stuck together” → low RF).
    • Farther apart genes → higher probability of crossing over (“easier to separate” → higher RF, maxing out at 50% where they behave as unlinked).
Recombination Frequency (RF) = (Total Recombinant Offspring / Total Offspring) × 100%
1% Recombination = 1 Map Unit (mu) = 1 Centimorgan (cM)
Spotting Linked Genes in Data Tables: In a dihybrid test cross (AaBb × aabb), unlinked genes yield a 1:1:1:1 ratio (25% each). If you see two huge classes (Parentals) and two tiny classes (Recombinants) (e.g., 420, 410, 85, 85), the genes are linked on the same chromosome!
Sex-Linked Inheritance (X-Linked & Y-Linked) Hemizygous Males
  • Sex Chromosomes in Humans: Females are XX (homogametic); Males are XY (heterogametic).
  • X-Linked Recessive Traits (e.g., red-green colorblindness, hemophilia A, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, Drosophila white eyes):
    • Males (XᵃY) are hemizygous: Having only one X chromosome, a single recessive allele is expressed directly!
    • Females (XᵃXᵃ) must inherit two recessive alleles (one from each parent) to express the trait; XᴬXᵃ females are unaffected carriers.
    • Key Hallmarks: Much more frequent in males; NO father-to-son transmission (fathers pass Y to sons, X to daughters); often skips generations via carrier mothers.
  • X-Linked Dominant: Single copy causes trait; affected father (XᴬY) passes trait to ALL daughters and NO sons.
  • Y-Linked (Holandric): Carried on Y chromosome (e.g., SRY gene) → affects only males; passed from father to 100% of sons.
Males Cannot Be X-Linked Carriers: Never refer to a male (XY) as a “carrier” of an X-linked trait — because males have only one X chromosome, they either show the phenotype or do not carry the allele at all!
Complex Allelic & Multi-Gene Inheritance Patterns Beyond Simple Dominance
Pattern Mechanism & Ratio Classic AP Biology Examples
Incomplete Dominance Neither allele is fully dominant; heterozygote shows an intermediate (blended) phenotype.
F₂ Phenotype = Genotype = 1 : 2 : 1		 Snapdragon flowers: Red (CᴿCᴿ) × White (CᵂCᵂ) → Pink (CᴿCᵂ)
Codominance Both alleles are fully and simultaneously expressed in the heterozygote (no blending). Human IᴬIᴮ (Type AB) blood expresses both A and B surface glycoproteins; roan cattle coat
Multiple Alleles >2 alleles exist in the population gene pool (though each diploid organism still carries only 2). ABO Blood Group: 3 alleles (Iᴬ, Iᴮ codominant; i recessive → 4 phenotypes: A, B, AB, O)
Pleiotropy 1 single gene affects multiple seemingly unrelated phenotypic traits across organ systems. Sickle cell disease (β-globin mutation alters RBC shape, oxygen transport, blood flow, organ damage & malaria resistance); Marfan syndrome
Epistasis Gene at one locus masks or alters phenotypic expression of a gene at a second locus.
Modifies 9:3:3:1 to 9 : 3 : 4 (or 9:7).		 Labrador Retriever coat color: B/b controls pigment (Black vs. Brown); homozygous recessive ee blocks pigment deposition → Yellow lab regardless of B_
Polygenic Inheritance Multiple genes have an additive effect on a single quantitative trait → continuous bell-curve distribution. Human height, skin pigmentation, eye color, intelligence
Don't Assume Mendelian Ratios Always Apply: Always inspect observed offspring ratios (and run χ²) before assuming standard 3:1 or 9:3:3:1 dominance!
Non-Nuclear (Cytoplasmic / Maternal) Inheritance Mitochondria & Chloroplasts
  • Genetic Basis: Mitochondria and chloroplasts (plastids) contain their own small circular DNA genomes (evidence for endosymbiosis) that replicate independently of nuclear chromosomes and do NOT undergo Mendelian segregation.
  • Strict Maternal Transmission:
    • During fertilization, the female ovum (egg) contributes virtually 100% of the zygote's cytoplasm and cytoplasmic organelles; the sperm contributes only a bare haploid nucleus.
    • Affected Mother → 100% of her offspring (both sons and daughters) inherit the trait!
    • Affected Father → 0% of his offspring inherit the trait!
  • Human Mitochondrial Myopathies / Leber's Hereditary Optic Neuropathy: Mutations in mitochondrial ETC/ATP synthase genes reduce cellular ATP production (severely impacting high-energy nervous and muscle tissues) and are passed strictly from mother to all children.
  • Variegated Leaf Color in Plants (e.g., Four O'Clock Mirabilis jalapa): Determined by maternal ovule chloroplast/plastid DNA, not pollen.
Pedigree Instant ID — X-Linked Dominant vs. Maternal Inheritance: In Maternal (mitochondrial) pedigrees, an affected mother passes the trait to every single child (100%), whereas an affected father passes it to none (0%).

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Investigation 7 Part 2 (Sordaria fimicola Meiotic Crossing Over) & Drosophila/Fast Plants Crosses: Counting 4:4 non-recombinant vs. 2:2:2:2 / 2:4:2 recombinant asci in Sordaria perithecia to determine gene-to-centromere map distance (% Crossover = [Recombinant asci / Total asci] × 100; Map distance in cM = % Crossover / 2 because only 2 of 4 chromatids in a tetrad cross over). Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative Formulas & Statistical Testing: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit: χ² = Σ [(O − E)² / E], with Degrees of Freedom df = (number of phenotypic classes − 1). At α = 0.05: if χ² > critical value, REJECT H₀ (e.g., genes are autosomal linked rather than assorting independently 9:3:3:1); if χ² ≤ critical value, FAIL TO REJECT H₀. Recombination Frequency: RF = (Total Recombinants / Total Offspring) × 100% = Map Units (cM). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Chromosomal Disruption Case Study: Meiotic Nondisjunction (failure of homologous chromosomes to separate in Anaphase I, or sister chromatids to separate in Anaphase II) yields aneuploid gametes (n + 1 or n − 1). Fertilization produces Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome, 47, +21), Klinefelter Syndrome (47, XXY), or monosomic Turner Syndrome (45, X0). Mitochondrial myopathies (e.g., Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy) exhibit strict maternal inheritance because the ovum contributes virtually all zygotic cytoplasm and mitochondria.
