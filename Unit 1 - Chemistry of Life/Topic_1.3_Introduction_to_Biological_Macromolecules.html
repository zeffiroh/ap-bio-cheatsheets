Monomers and Polymers: Modular Architecture Covalent Linkages
  • Monomers: Small organic molecular subunits that serve as building blocks.
  • Polymers: Large macromolecules constructed by linking repeating monomers together via strong covalent bonds.
  • Polymerization Math: Linking n monomers into a linear polymer requires n − 1 covalent bonds and releases n − 1 molecules of H₂O.
Macromolecule Class Monomer / Subunit Covalent Linkage Name
Carbohydrates (Polysaccharides) Monosaccharides (e.g., Glucose) Glycosidic linkage (–C–O–C–)
Proteins (Polypeptides) Amino acids (20 types) Peptide bond (–CO–NH–)
Nucleic Acids (DNA / RNA) Nucleotides Phosphodiester bond (5′→3′)
Lipids (non-polymeric) Glycerol + Fatty acids Ester linkage (–COO–)
Monosaccharides → polysaccharides (starch/glycogen); amino acids → proteins (polypeptides); nucleotides → nucleic acids (DNA/RNA).
Polymer = “beads on a string” (monomers = individual beads; covalent bonds = the string connecting them).
Dehydration Synthesis (Condensation Reaction) Bond Formation
  • Core Principle: Water removed (−H₂O) → new covalent bond formed (Monomers → Polymer).
  • Repeated Dehydration Reactions: Extending a growing polymer chain adds one monomer at a time; each covalent bond formed releases 1 H₂O.
  • Chemical Mechanism (Enzyme-Catalyzed):
    • Remove a hydrogen atom (–H / H⁺) from the functional group of one monomer.
    • Remove a hydroxyl group (–OH / OH⁻) from the adjacent monomer.
    • H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O is released as a byproduct, and the two monomers share electrons to form a new covalent bond.
Dehydration = “glue reaction” (removes water to stick monomers together into a polymer).
Hydrolysis: Cleaving Polymers with Water Bond Breaking
  • Core Principle: Water added (+H₂O) → covalent bond broken (Polymer → smaller units / individual monomers). Hydro = water, lysis = to split.
  • Chemical Mechanism:
    • A water molecule splits into H₂O → H⁺ + OH⁻ across the covalent linkage.
    • H⁺ (–H) attaches to one released monomer.
    • OH⁻ (–OH) attaches to the other monomer, restoring both functional groups.
  • Biological Roles of Hydrolysis:
    • Digestion in the GI Tract: Hydrolytic enzymes (amylases, proteases, lipases, nucleases) break ingested food macromolecules into absorbable monomers:
      • Carbohydrates → monosaccharides (glucose)
      • Proteins → amino acids
      • Lipids → fatty acids + glycerol
      • Nucleic acids → nucleotides
    • Intracellular Recycling (Autophagy / Lysosomes): Breaks worn-out cellular macromolecules back into reusable monomers to build new cell structures.
Energetics & ATP Coupling in Polymerization Thermodynamics
  • Dehydration Synthesis = Endergonic (ΔG > 0):
    • Anabolic assembly increases molecular order (decreases entropy, −ΔS) and requires free energy input (usually coupled to ATP hydrolysis).
  • Hydrolysis = Exergonic (ΔG < 0):
    • Catabolic cleavage breaks polymers into smaller units, increasing entropy (+ΔS) and releasing free energy.
  • Energy Coupling via ATP Hydrolysis:
    • ATP itself is broken down by hydrolysis:
ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pᵢ + Free Energy (ΔG = −30.5 kJ/mol)
  • That released free energy from ATP hydrolysis powers endergonic dehydration synthesis reactions (e.g., protein translation at ribosomes, DNA replication, glycogen synthesis).
Yes, it’s counterintuitive at the macromolecular level: Building biological polymers via dehydration synthesis costs energy (endergonic), whereas hydrolyzing macromolecules into monomers releases energy (exergonic)!
Stoichiometry Calculation Trap: If an AP question asks for the chemical formula of a trisaccharide made of 3 hexose sugars (C₆H₁₂O₆), do NOT simply multiply by 3 (C₁₈H₃₆O₁₈)! Subtract 2 H₂O (n − 1 = 2 bonds formed) → correct formula is C₁₈H₃₂O₁₆.

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Lab Context: Macromolecule Indicator Assays (Benedict's for reducing sugars, Lugol's Iodine for starch, Biuret for peptide bonds) & Buffer Titration Curves. Independent Variable (IV): pH or temperature treatment; Dependent Variable (DV): absorbance/colorimetric change or structural stability; Negative Control: deionized H₂O. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Stoichiometric monomer-to-polymer condensation: for n monomers joined in a linear chain, (n − 1) covalent bonds form and (n − 1) H₂O molecules are released. pH scale is logarithmic: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; each 1-unit decrease in pH represents a 10-fold increase in [H⁺]. Caloric density: Lipids (~9 kcal/g) vs. Carbohydrates/Proteins (~4 kcal/g). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Missense mutation in the β-globin gene (Glu6Val in Sickle-Cell Disease) replaces a polar/charged glutamic acid R-group with a nonpolar hydrophobic valine on the protein exterior, driving abnormal quaternary hydrophobic aggregation into rigid fibers under deoxygenated conditions. Similarly, extreme fever (>40°C) or acidosis (pH < 7.0) disrupts weak hydrogen and ionic bonds in tertiary (3°) protein structure while leaving primary (1°) peptide bonds intact.
