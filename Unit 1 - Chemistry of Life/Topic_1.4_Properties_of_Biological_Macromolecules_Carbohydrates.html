Monosaccharides (Simple Sugars) (CH₂O)ₙ • 1:2:1 Ratio
  • Smallest Carbohydrate Units: Monomers with general empirical formula (CH₂O)ₙ (carbon:hydrogen:oxygen in a 1 : 2 : 1 ratio).
  • Chemical Structure:
    • Carbon Backbone: 3 to 7 carbons (trioses = 3C like G3P; pentoses = 5C like ribose/deoxyribose; hexoses = 6C like glucose).
    • Multiple Hydroxyl Groups (–OH): Make sugars polar, hydrophilic, and highly water-soluble via H-bonding.
    • One Carbonyl Group (C=O):
      • Aldose (carbonyl at end of carbon chain) → e.g., Glucose, Galactose.
      • Ketose (carbonyl in middle of carbon chain) → e.g., Fructose.
  • In aqueous solutions, 5C and 6C sugars spontaneously cyclize into stable ring structures.
Glucose, fructose, and galactose are all hexose monosaccharides with the identical molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆.
Isomers & Disaccharides (Same Formula, Different Structure) Specificity
  • Isomers: Molecules with the same molecular formula (C₆H₁₂O₆) but different covalent or spatial arrangements → leads to distinct biological recognition by enzymes.
    • Structural Isomers: Different covalent bonding order (e.g., glucose [aldose] vs. fructose [ketose]).
    • Stereoisomers (Geometric / Optical): Same covalent partnerships, different 3D spatial orientation of –OH groups (e.g., glucose vs. galactose at C4; α-glucose vs. β-glucose at C1).
  • Common Disaccharides (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁): Formed by dehydration synthesis of 2 monosaccharides:
    • Maltose (malt sugar) = Glucose + Glucose
    • Sucrose (table sugar / phloem transport) = Glucose + Fructose
    • Lactose (milk sugar) = Glucose + Galactose
Glucose vs. Galactose differ only in the 3D orientation of a single –OH group on carbon-4, yet require completely different enzymes for metabolism!
Polysaccharides: Structure Determines Function Complex Carbs
  • Polysaccharides: Long polymers of hundreds to thousands of monosaccharides joined by covalent glycosidic linkages (1→4 linear chains or 1→6 branch points) formed via dehydration synthesis.
  • Branching vs. Linearity Dictates Biological Role:
    • Linear Chains (β-1,4 linkages): Form straight, unbranched rigid rods where parallel chains cross-link via thousands of inter-chain hydrogen bonds → high tensile strength for structural support.
    • Helical & Branched Chains (α-1,4 + α-1,6 linkages): Compact, bushy helical granules → osmotically inert energy storage with many free non-reducing ends for rapid enzymatic hydrolysis.
Keyword Alert: Always state that glycosidic bond = covalent sugar–sugar linkage, and that more branches = more free ends for enzymes to hydrolyze glucose simultaneously → faster ATP mobilization!
Storage vs. Structural Polysaccharides 4 Major Polymers
Polysaccharide Monomer & Bond Branching & Biological Role
Starch (Plants) α-glucose (α-1,4) Unbranched (amylose) or moderately branched (amylopectin); energy storage in plastids/roots.
Glycogen (Animals) α-glucose (α-1,4 & α-1,6) Extensively branched; stored in liver & skeletal muscle for rapid glucose mobilization.
Cellulose (Plants) β-glucose (β-1,4) Unbranched straight chains (every 2nd β-glucose flipped 180°); H-bonded microfibrils in plant cell walls.
Chitin (Fungi/Arthropods) β-N-acetylglucosamine Contains Nitrogen (N); structural support in fungal cell walls & arthropod exoskeletons.
Digestibility & Enzyme Specificity: Humans possess salivary/pancreatic α-amylase to hydrolyze α-1,4 bonds in starch & glycogen, but cannot hydrolyze β-1,4 linkages in cellulose (dietary fiber). Herbivores (cows, termites) rely on symbiotic gut microbes producing cellulase.
Mixing α vs. β glucose:
• α-glucose → Helical & branched energy storage (Starch & Glycogen)
• β-glucose → Straight, H-bonded structural fibers (Cellulose & Chitin)
AP CED Scope Note: Memorizing the exact carbon-by-carbon molecular structures of specific carbohydrate polymers is beyond the scope of the AP Exam — focus on monomer orientation, branching vs. linearity, and functional consequences!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Lab Context: Macromolecule Indicator Assays (Benedict's for reducing sugars, Lugol's Iodine for starch, Biuret for peptide bonds) & Buffer Titration Curves. Independent Variable (IV): pH or temperature treatment; Dependent Variable (DV): absorbance/colorimetric change or structural stability; Negative Control: deionized H₂O. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Stoichiometric monomer-to-polymer condensation: for n monomers joined in a linear chain, (n − 1) covalent bonds form and (n − 1) H₂O molecules are released. pH scale is logarithmic: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; each 1-unit decrease in pH represents a 10-fold increase in [H⁺]. Caloric density: Lipids (~9 kcal/g) vs. Carbohydrates/Proteins (~4 kcal/g). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Missense mutation in the β-globin gene (Glu6Val in Sickle-Cell Disease) replaces a polar/charged glutamic acid R-group with a nonpolar hydrophobic valine on the protein exterior, driving abnormal quaternary hydrophobic aggregation into rigid fibers under deoxygenated conditions. Similarly, extreme fever (>40°C) or acidosis (pH < 7.0) disrupts weak hydrogen and ionic bonds in tertiary (3°) protein structure while leaving primary (1°) peptide bonds intact.
