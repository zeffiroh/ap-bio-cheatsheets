General Properties & Fatty Acid Structure Hydrophobic C–H
  • Defining Characteristic: Grouped together NOT by repeating monomer polymers, but because they are mostly nonpolar → hydrophobic (insoluble in water).
  • Elemental Makeup: Built from long hydrocarbon chains (C–H bonds) with very little oxygen (H:O ratio ≫ 2:1).
  • 3 Major Biological Classes: Fats (triglycerides), phospholipids, and steroids.
  • Fatty Acid Components:
    • Carboxyl Group (–COOH): Acidic, polar head at one end.
    • Hydrocarbon Tail (–(CH₂)ₙ–CH₃): Long nonpolar chain (typically 14–22 carbons); often makes lipids amphipathic (having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions).
Lipids = “water-fearing molecules” (nonpolar C–H chains cannot form hydrogen bonds with water and are excluded by water's H-bond network).
Saturated vs. Unsaturated Fatty Acids Membrane Fluidity
Feature Saturated Fatty Acids Unsaturated Fatty Acids
C–C Bonding Only single C–C bonds (saturated with max H atoms) ≥1 cis double bond (C=C) (fewer H atoms)
Chain Geometry Straight chains → pack tightly via van der Waals forces Bent / kinked chains at cis C=C → prevent tight packing
Room Temp State Solid at room temp (e.g., butter, lard, animal fats) Liquid at room temp (e.g., olive oil, plant & fish oils)
Membrane Effect Decreases fluidity (more viscous / rigid membrane) Increases fluidity (stays fluid at cold temperatures)
Butter (saturated animal fat, solid) vs. olive oil (unsaturated plant oil, liquid); cold-water fish & winter wheat increase unsaturated fatty acid % in membranes so membranes don't solidify in freezing water!
Saturated = straight spaghetti → packs tightly in the box.
Unsaturated = kinked spaghetti → loose packing with space between tails.
Always use the exact phrase: “cis double bonds (C=C) introduce kinks in hydrocarbon tails, preventing tight packing and increasing membrane fluidity.”
Fats (Triglycerides / Triacylglycerols) ~9 kcal/g Energy
  • Structure: 1 Glycerol (3-carbon alcohol with 3 –OH groups) covalently linked to 3 Fatty Acid chains via 3 ester linkages (formed by 3 dehydration synthesis reactions releasing 3 H₂O).
  • Biological Functions:
    • Long-Term Energy Storage: Dense, anhydrous (stores without heavy hydration water).
    • Thermal Insulation: Subcutaneous adipose tissue (blubber in whales/seals).
    • Mechanical Protection: Cushions vital internal organs (kidneys, heart).
  • Energy Yield: ~9 kcal/g (more than double the ~4 kcal/g of carbohydrates or proteins) due to abundant reduced, high-energy C–H bonds.
Don't confuse energy density with speed: Carbohydrates (4 kcal/g) are for quick, immediate ATP; Fats (9 kcal/g) store more than double the energy per gram for long-term reserves!
Phospholipids & Bilayer Self-Assembly Amphipathic
  • Phospholipid Structure: 1 Glycerol + 2 Fatty Acid tails + 1 negatively charged Phosphate group (–PO₄²⁻) (often linked to a polar choline/serine group).
  • Amphipathic Nature:
    • Hydrophilic Head (glycerol + phosphate + polar group) = polar/charged → interacts with water.
    • Hydrophobic Tails (2 hydrocarbon fatty acid chains, often 1 saturated + 1 kinked unsaturated) = nonpolar → excluded from water.
  • Phospholipid Bilayer (Spontaneous Assembly in Water):
    • Driven by hydrophobic interactions: hydrophilic heads face outward toward aqueous cytosol & extracellular fluid; hydrophobic tails face inward away from water.
    • Emergent Membrane Properties: Selective permeability (blocks ions/polar solutes, allows small nonpolar molecules), lateral fluidity, and spontaneous self-healing.
Bilayer = Sandwich: Bread = hydrophilic heads facing water outside; Filling = hydrophobic fatty acid tails tucked inside.
Always link: Amphipathic nature → spontaneous phospholipid bilayer formation in aqueous environments!
Steroids: Cholesterol & Steroid Signaling 4 Fused Carbon Rings
  • Steroid Structure: Characterized by a rigid carbon skeleton of four fused carbon rings (3 six-membered rings + 1 five-membered ring) with varied functional groups attached.
  • Cholesterol (Animal Cell Membrane Buffer):
    • Wedges between phospholipid tails in animal plasma membranes and acts as a bidirectional temperature fluidity buffer:
      • At High Temperatures: Restrains phospholipid movement → ↓ decreases fluidity (prevents melting/excessive permeability).
      • At Low Temperatures: Disrupts tight packing of saturated tails → ↑ increases fluidity (prevents freezing/solidification).
  • Steroid Hormones (Estrogen, Testosterone, Cortisol, Aldosterone):
    • Because they are nonpolar and lipid-soluble (hydrophobic), steroid hormones diffuse directly across the phospholipid bilayer without transport proteins.
    • Bind to intracellular (cytoplasmic or nuclear) receptors inside the target cell, forming a hormone-receptor complex that acts as a transcription factor to directly regulate gene expression!
Confusing steroid vs. protein/peptide hormones:
• Steroid hormones (estrogen, testosterone) are nonpolar → cross the plasma membrane and bind intracellular receptors.
• Protein/peptide hormones (insulin, epinephrine) are polar/charged → cannot cross the hydrophobic core and must bind cell-surface membrane receptors!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Lab Context: Macromolecule Indicator Assays (Benedict's for reducing sugars, Lugol's Iodine for starch, Biuret for peptide bonds) & Buffer Titration Curves. Independent Variable (IV): pH or temperature treatment; Dependent Variable (DV): absorbance/colorimetric change or structural stability; Negative Control: deionized H₂O. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Stoichiometric monomer-to-polymer condensation: for n monomers joined in a linear chain, (n − 1) covalent bonds form and (n − 1) H₂O molecules are released. pH scale is logarithmic: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; each 1-unit decrease in pH represents a 10-fold increase in [H⁺]. Caloric density: Lipids (~9 kcal/g) vs. Carbohydrates/Proteins (~4 kcal/g). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Missense mutation in the β-globin gene (Glu6Val in Sickle-Cell Disease) replaces a polar/charged glutamic acid R-group with a nonpolar hydrophobic valine on the protein exterior, driving abnormal quaternary hydrophobic aggregation into rigid fibers under deoxygenated conditions. Similarly, extreme fever (>40°C) or acidosis (pH < 7.0) disrupts weak hydrogen and ionic bonds in tertiary (3°) protein structure while leaving primary (1°) peptide bonds intact.
