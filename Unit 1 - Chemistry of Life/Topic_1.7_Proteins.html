Amino Acids & Peptide Bond Formation N-Terminus → C-Terminus
  • Monomers = Amino Acids (20 standard types): Every amino acid shares an identical backbone bonded to a central asymmetric alpha-carbon (α-C):
    • Amino Group (–NH₂ / –NH₃⁺): Basic N-terminus end.
    • Carboxyl Group (–COOH / –COO⁻): Acidic C-terminus end.
    • Hydrogen Atom (–H) + a variable R Group (Side Chain).
  • Peptide Bonds (Strong Covalent Linkage):
    • Formed via dehydration synthesis at the ribosome: the carboxyl group (–COOH) of one amino acid reacts with the amino group (–NH₂) of the next amino acid, releasing 1 H₂O.
    • Creates a directional polypeptide chain synthesized from N-terminus (–NH₂) → C-terminus (–COOH).
Peptide bond = “link between beads”; always write or state: “a covalent peptide bond forms between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next via a dehydration reaction.”
Confusing peptide bonds with hydrogen bonds: Peptide bonds are strong covalent bonds that hold the primary backbone together (NOT broken by heat/pH denaturation!).
R Groups (Side Chains) Determine Folding Behavior Chemical Personality
  • While the N–C–C backbone is identical in all amino acids, the variable R group dictates chemical properties and 3D folding:
    • Nonpolar (Hydrophobic) R-Groups: Rich in –CH₃ / –CH₂– hydrocarbons → avoid water and cluster on the interior core of cytosolic proteins (or face lipid tails in transmembrane domains).
    • Polar Uncharged (Hydrophilic) R-Groups: Contain –OH, –NH₂, or –SH → form hydrogen bonds with water or other polar R-groups on the protein exterior.
    • Charged (Ionic: Acidic –COO⁻ or Basic –NH₃⁺) R-Groups: Hydrophilic; attract opposite charges to form ionic bonds (salt bridges).
    • Sulfhydryl (–SH in Cysteine): Two Cysteine side chains oxidize to form a covalent disulfide bridge (–S–S–).
R groups = “personality of amino acids” (dictates whether that region folds inward away from water or outward toward water).
Mutation FRQ Strategy: When an AP question asks how a missense mutation affects a protein, compare the R-group chemistry! Replacing a hydrophobic amino acid with another hydrophobic one may be silent/neutral, whereas replacing a hydrophilic/charged R-group with a nonpolar R-group alters 3° folding and destroys active-site function!
The 4 Levels of Protein Structure (1° → 4°) Hierarchy of Folding
Level Structural Definition Stabilizing Bonds / Interactions
Primary (1°) Unique linear sequence of amino acids (N-terminus → C-terminus), encoded by mRNA codons. Covalent peptide bonds (–CO–NH–) between backbone monomers.
Secondary (2°) Local regular coils & folds: α-helix (spiral) and β-pleated sheet (parallel/antiparallel ribbons). Hydrogen bonds along the polypeptide backbone (C=O oxygen to N–H hydrogen; NOT R-groups!).
Tertiary (3°) Overall 3D folded conformation of a single polypeptide chain (creates enzyme active sites). R-group interactions: Hydrophobic clustering, H-bonds, ionic salt bridges, & covalent disulfide bridges (–S–S–).
Quaternary (4°) Assembly of ≥2 polypeptide subunits into one functional multimeric protein complex. Same noncovalent & disulfide interactions as 3°, acting between separate subunits.
• Sickle-Cell Anemia (1° → 4° Cascade): A single amino acid substitution at position 6 of β-globin replaces hydrophilic Glutamic Acid (charged) with hydrophobic Valine (nonpolar) → abnormal hydrophobic patch causes hemoglobin tetramers (4°) to aggregate into rigid fibers that sickle red blood cells.
• 3° & 4° Examples: Enzyme active sites depend on precise 3° folding; Hemoglobin (2 α + 2 β subunits) & Collagen (3 helical chains) exhibit 4° structure.
Structure–Function & Protein Denaturation Active Site Loss
  • Core Dogma of Protein Biology:
    DNA Base Sequence → 1° Amino Acid Sequence → 3D Folding (2°/3°/4°) → Specific Function
  • Major Protein Functions: Enzymatic catalysis (lowers Eₐ), structural support (keratin, collagen), membrane transport (channels/pumps), cell signaling (receptors, insulin), defense (antibodies), and motility (actin/myosin).
  • Denaturation (Loss of Native 3D Shape & Function):
    • Triggers: Extreme high temperature (thermal agitation breaks H-bonds/hydrophobic interactions), extreme pH shifts (H⁺ / OH⁻ alters R-group charges, disrupting ionic salt bridges), or high salinity/urea.
    • What Breaks vs. What Survives: Denaturation disrupts weak 2°, 3°, and 4° interactions (unfolding the active site), while 1° covalent peptide bonds remain intact! Sometimes reversible (renaturation) if gentle, or permanent (e.g., boiling an egg).
The 3-Link FRQ Chain: Whenever explaining mutations or environmental shifts in proteins, you MUST link all three: 1) Primary sequence / R-group interactions → 2) 3D folding / active site shape → 3) Biological function / catalytic activity!
AP CED Scope Note: Memorizing the exact chemical structures of the 20 individual amino acid R-groups is NOT required on the AP Exam — you will be given structures or properties and asked to classify them as nonpolar, polar, or charged/ionic!

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Lab Context: Macromolecule Indicator Assays (Benedict's for reducing sugars, Lugol's Iodine for starch, Biuret for peptide bonds) & Buffer Titration Curves. Independent Variable (IV): pH or temperature treatment; Dependent Variable (DV): absorbance/colorimetric change or structural stability; Negative Control: deionized H₂O. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Stoichiometric monomer-to-polymer condensation: for n monomers joined in a linear chain, (n − 1) covalent bonds form and (n − 1) H₂O molecules are released. pH scale is logarithmic: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; each 1-unit decrease in pH represents a 10-fold increase in [H⁺]. Caloric density: Lipids (~9 kcal/g) vs. Carbohydrates/Proteins (~4 kcal/g). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Missense mutation in the β-globin gene (Glu6Val in Sickle-Cell Disease) replaces a polar/charged glutamic acid R-group with a nonpolar hydrophobic valine on the protein exterior, driving abnormal quaternary hydrophobic aggregation into rigid fibers under deoxygenated conditions. Similarly, extreme fever (>40°C) or acidosis (pH < 7.0) disrupts weak hydrogen and ionic bonds in tertiary (3°) protein structure while leaving primary (1°) peptide bonds intact.
