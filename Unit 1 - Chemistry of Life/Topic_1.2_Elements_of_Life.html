Major Bioelements (C, H, O, N, P, S) 99% of Biomass
  • Living systems require a constant exchange of matter with the environment to synthesize 4 classes of biological macromolecules.
  • Core Triad (C, H, O): Most abundant elements in organisms; form the structural framework of carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
  • Biogeochemical Cycling: Organisms assimilate inorganic forms (CO₂, H₂O, NH₄⁺ / NO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻, SO₄²⁻) into organic monomers.
Carbon (C): The Versatile Backbone of Life 4 Valence e⁻
  • Tetravalent: Has 4 valence electrons in its outer shell → forms up to 4 stable covalent bonds (single, double, or triple).
  • Structural Diversity: Forms straight chains, branched chains, and closed rings (isomers with identical formulas but distinct functions).
  • Bonds Readily With: H, O, N, S, and P — high C–C bond dissociation energy (~348 kJ/mol) provides thermodynamic stability + conformational flexibility.
Glucose rings (C₆H₁₂O₆), fatty acid hydrocarbon chains, and steroid 4-ring skeletons demonstrate carbon's structural versatility.
Hydrogen (H) & Oxygen (O): Energy & Polarity Redox & H-Bonds
  • Hydrogen (H) — Structure + Chemical Energy:
    • Forms 1 single covalent bond (1 valence e⁻).
    • Hydrogen Bonding: When bonded to electronegative O or N, carries a partial positive charge (δ⁺) that stabilizes DNA base pairs & protein folding.
    • Energy Storage in C–H Bonds: Nonpolar C–H bonds hold high-potential electrons; high H:O ratio → greater free energy yield upon oxidation.
  • Oxygen (O) — Electronegativity + Energy Transfer:
    • Highly Electronegative (3.44): Pulls shared electrons toward itself (δ⁻), creating polar covalent bonds (hydrophilicity & reactive functional groups).
    • Cellular Respiration Role: Acts as the terminal electron acceptor in the mitochondrial ETC (½ O₂ + 2e⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O), driving oxidative phosphorylation.
Lipids have long nonpolar C–H chains (low O content) → store ~9 kcal/g vs. carbohydrates/proteins at ~4 kcal/g.
Macromolecules & Diagnostic Elemental Signatures High-Yield Radioisotope Table
Macromolecule Elements Diagnostic Ratio / Isotope Marker
Carbohydrates C, H, O 1 : 2 : 1 stoichiometric ratio (CH₂O)ₙ; rich in polar -OH groups
Lipids C, H, O (+ P in phospholipids) High C & H, very little O (H:O ≫ 2:1); nonpolar & hydrophobic
Proteins C, H, O, N, S N in every amino group; ³⁵S uniquely labels proteins (Cysteine/Methionine)
Nucleic Acids C, H, O, N, P N in bases; ³²P labels sugar-phosphate backbone (no S present)
Hershey-Chase Experiment Application: AP questions frequently ask how to distinguish DNA from protein using radioactive tracers — use ³²P for DNA (phosphodiester backbone) and ³⁵S for Protein (disulfide-forming amino acids)!
Nitrogen (N): Genetic Information & Polypeptides 3 Covalent Bonds
  • Essential Building Block Of:
    • Amino Acids & Proteins: Forms the amino group (-NH₂ / -NH₃⁺) and links monomers via peptide bonds (-CO–NH-).
    • Nitrogenous Bases (DNA, RNA, ATP): Forms aromatic rings in Purines (Adenine, Guanine — 2 rings) and Pyrimidines (Cytosine, Thymine, Uracil — 1 ring).
  • Enables Base Pairing: Electronegative N atoms participate in specific hydrogen bonds between complementary antiparallel strands, enabling faithful genetic replication and transcription.
Complementary Base Pairing: A–T / A–U form 2 H-bonds; G–C forms 3 H-bonds (higher G–C % = higher DNA thermal stability / melting temp Tₘ).
Phosphorus (P): Membranes, Nucleic Acids & Energy PO₄³⁻ Group
  • Found biologically as the negatively charged phosphate group (-PO₄²⁻), which imparts high water solubility and negative charge at cellular pH (~7.4).
  • In Phospholipids: Forms the charged, hydrophilic polar head that faces aqueous cytosol/extracellular fluid, driving spontaneous lipid bilayer self-assembly.
  • In Nucleic Acids (DNA/RNA): Links the 3′ carbon of one pentose sugar to the 5′ carbon of the next via covalent phosphodiester bonds, creating the directional 5′ → 3′ sugar-phosphate backbone (gives DNA its net negative charge for gel electrophoresis!).
  • In Cellular Energetics: Forms high-energy phosphoanhydride bonds in ATP & regulates enzymes via phosphorylation.
Sulfur (S): Tertiary Protein Stability Disulfide Bridges
  • Found Exclusively in Proteins: Present in the R-groups of two amino acids — Cysteine (contains reactive sulfhydryl -SH) and Methionine (coded by start codon AUG).
  • Covalent Disulfide Bridges (–S–S–): Oxidation of two -SH groups forms a strong covalent cross-link between distant regions of a polypeptide chain.
  • Functional Role: Locks 3D tertiary (3°) and quaternary (4°) protein conformation in place, resisting thermal or pH denaturation.
Human Insulin consists of two polypeptide chains (A & B) covalently cross-linked and stabilized by 3 disulfide bridges (–S–S–); curly hair keratin is also rich in disulfide bonds.
  • C → Versatile 4-bond structural backbone (chains, branches, rings)
  • H → Chemical energy storage (C–H bonds) & H-bonding (δ⁺)
  • O → High electronegativity / polarity (δ⁻) & final e⁻ acceptor
  • N → Genetic info (purines/pyrimidines) & proteins (amino groups)
  • P → DNA/RNA 5′→3′ backbone, ATP, & amphipathic membranes

I. Experimental Design & Required Lab Controls AP Lab Context: Macromolecule Indicator Assays (Benedict's for reducing sugars, Lugol's Iodine for starch, Biuret for peptide bonds) & Buffer Titration Curves. Independent Variable (IV): pH or temperature treatment; Dependent Variable (DV): absorbance/colorimetric change or structural stability; Negative Control: deionized H₂O. Null Hypothesis (H₀): State explicitly that the independent variable treatment has no statistically significant effect on the dependent variable; any observed difference is due to random sampling chance alone.
II. Quantitative Formulas & Graph Interpretation Quantitative & Graph Analysis: Stoichiometric monomer-to-polymer condensation: for n monomers joined in a linear chain, (n − 1) covalent bonds form and (n − 1) H₂O molecules are released. pH scale is logarithmic: pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; each 1-unit decrease in pH represents a 10-fold increase in [H⁺]. Caloric density: Lipids (~9 kcal/g) vs. Carbohydrates/Proteins (~4 kcal/g). Error Bar Rule (±2 SE_x̄): If the ±2 SE_x̄ error bars of two treatment groups do not overlap, there is a statistically significant difference between means (p < 0.05; reject H₀).
III. Pathology, Mutation & Disruption Case Study Clinical & Molecular Disruption Case Study: Missense mutation in the β-globin gene (Glu6Val in Sickle-Cell Disease) replaces a polar/charged glutamic acid R-group with a nonpolar hydrophobic valine on the protein exterior, driving abnormal quaternary hydrophobic aggregation into rigid fibers under deoxygenated conditions. Similarly, extreme fever (>40°C) or acidosis (pH < 7.0) disrupts weak hydrogen and ionic bonds in tertiary (3°) protein structure while leaving primary (1°) peptide bonds intact.
